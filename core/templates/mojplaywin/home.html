01 / ORIGINAL GAME DEVELOPMENT · ORIGINAL GUIDE

From a game idea to a playable vertical slice

A promising game pitch becomes useful only when players can make decisions inside a working prototype. A vertical slice is a short, complete piece of gameplay demonstrating the core loop, controls, visual language and feedback—not a large collection of disconnected assets.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Define one central player action and the consequence that makes it interesting. Build a five-minute playable scenario with a clear start, success condition and failure state. Measure time to understand controls, completion without hints and willingness to replay. Freeze cosmetic additions while the team resolves input delay and unclear rules.

Illustrative scenario

For a strategy game, the slice may contain a small map, two resource choices and a single opponent. A tester should be able to explain the trade-off between expansion and defense. If they cannot, extra levels will multiply the problem rather than solve it.

Quality checklist

  1. One complete interaction loop
  2. Short usability test with fresh players
  3. Documented build, known defects and next decision

02 / INCLUSIVE GAME DESIGN · ORIGINAL GUIDE

Accessible controls make games better for more players

Accessibility is a design quality standard, not an optional feature for a later release. People play with touch, keyboard, controllers, assistive technology and different sensory abilities. A game should communicate important information through more than color or sound alone.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Provide remappable controls where relevant, readable contrast, scalable interface text, subtitles for significant dialogue and a visible pause mechanism. Allow players to reduce nonessential animation. In menus, ensure keyboard focus is visible and important actions do not rely on a hover effect that cannot exist on touchscreens.

Illustrative scenario

If a puzzle uses red and green pieces to convey opposing teams, add clearly different shapes or icons so the rule remains understandable for a player with color-vision differences. Test whether the same instructions remain usable on a small display at increased text size.

Quality checklist

  1. Test keyboard, touch and focus paths
  2. Use captions and noncolor indicators
  3. Offer clear motion and sound preferences

03 / APPLIED AI OPERATIONS · ORIGINAL GUIDE

Build useful AI assistants around measurable tasks

The starting point for a professional AI assistant is the user's problem, not the number of model features. A reliable assistant should accept defined inputs, use approved evidence, produce an inspectable output and communicate what it could not verify.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Choose a single repeatable business task, such as classifying support tickets or summarizing approved policy documents. Write acceptance cases with correct outputs and failure cases. Set limits on data access, external tool use and autonomous actions. Compare performance with an existing manual workflow and document human review requirements.

Illustrative scenario

A support-triage assistant can label incoming requests by category and urgency, but it should not promise refunds or disclose account details without verified permission. Pilot it on a small set of anonymized examples, review disagreements and only then consider deploying it on actual tickets.

Quality checklist

  1. Task-specific acceptance criteria
  2. Verified evidence and restricted tools
  3. Human-reviewed pilot before expansion

04 / DATA PROTECTION · ORIGINAL GUIDE

Protect customer data throughout an AI workflow

AI applications often handle sensitive business material even when the feature appears simple. Data protection depends on what is collected, where it travels, how long it remains available and whether administrators can understand or remove it.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Draw a data-flow map covering uploads, embeddings, prompts, generated outputs, backups and external providers. Minimize identifiers before processing and apply access checks at retrieval time. Establish a retention period and a clear process for removing customer material. Audit logs should demonstrate actions without unnecessarily reproducing entire private documents.

Illustrative scenario

If a customer uploads a supplier contract for comparison, the assistant may need the commercial terms but not the personal phone numbers or private signatures. Redact or restrict irrelevant content and test that another customer cannot retrieve the file by changing a document identifier.

Quality checklist

  1. Data inventory and retention policy
  2. Document-level access enforcement
  3. Tests for cross-user data isolation

05 / DIGITAL STUDIO · ORIGINAL GUIDE

How to create a credible digital-product demonstration

A compelling demo shows real product behavior and helps customers judge whether it solves their problem. Cinematic motion may attract attention, but it cannot replace a demonstration of the actual workflow, supported formats and limitations.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Structure a short demonstration around the customer's task: the starting problem, the input, a visible product action and the resulting output. Label simulated or conceptual scenes clearly. Prepare a shot list and captions before recording. Keep claims consistent with tested product functionality and make the final video understandable without sound.

Illustrative scenario

For a budgeting application, show the user entering input costs, selecting a scenario and reading a generated margin comparison. If a video segment is illustrative animation rather than a working screen capture, identify it as a concept so viewers are not misled.

Quality checklist

  1. One truthful use case demonstrated
  2. Legible captions and clear transitions
  3. Verified claims and appropriate media rights

06 / DIGITAL PRODUCTS · ORIGINAL GUIDE

A buyer-readiness checklist for downloadable products

Before offering an ebook, software file or professional template for sale, confirm that the customer understands exactly what they receive. A completed download is not the same as a usable, supported product; delivery, compatibility, instructions and recovery all affect customer satisfaction.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Prepare a product record with intended audience, actual file format, version, content outline, known limitations, license and requirements. Test the final package on more than one device. Where checkout is active, verify invoice creation, payment confirmation, entitlement issuance and successful retrieval using a controlled purchase rather than relying only on unit tests.

Illustrative scenario

An interactive planning workbook may open perfectly on the developer's computer but show broken formulas on an older office suite. A release test should include a clean installation, sample input data, expected calculation and an export or print check. If verified checkout is not available, label the product a preview rather than showing a misleading purchase button.

Quality checklist

  1. Descriptive specifications and honest availability
  2. Cross-device file and data validation
  3. Tested delivery, license and support journey

07 / ENTERPRISE WEB DEVELOPMENT · ORIGINAL GUIDE

The discovery workshop that prevents expensive web rebuilds

A corporate web project can fail long before the first line of code is written. Conflicting expectations about users, content ownership, languages, integrations and operational responsibility often turn a visually polished site into an expensive maintenance burden.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Gather representatives from business, content, security and support teams for a structured discovery session. Record critical user journeys and success criteria, such as finding a technical specification or submitting a distributor inquiry. Inventory existing content and integrations. Confirm hosting constraints, traffic needs, accessibility expectations and a plan for safe migration.

Illustrative scenario

For an industrial manufacturer, the most valuable first release may be a searchable multilingual technical catalog rather than a complex custom dashboard. Test whether buyers find relevant documents and reach a valid contact form. Add automation only after this journey works and the data source is reliable.

Quality checklist

  1. Prioritized user journeys and content owners
  2. Integration and security boundaries
  3. Staged release with acceptance and rollback criteria

08 / CUSTOMER OPERATIONS · ORIGINAL GUIDE

Turn repeated support questions into a useful knowledge base

Customers often ask the same questions about compatibility, accounts, payment confirmation and downloaded files. A carefully maintained help library shortens resolution time and makes products easier to use without promising a level of support the team cannot provide.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Review genuine support requests, group them by user intent and write short solution articles that start with the observable symptom. Give each article a tested procedure, supported version, warning about irreversible actions and a way to contact support if the steps fail. Measure whether readers can resolve the issue, not merely whether an article received views.

Illustrative scenario

A help article for an ebook that will not open should specify supported readers, download completeness and safe redownload options. It should avoid asking users to share passwords, wallet seed phrases or private account screenshots. If a file defect is widespread, fix the source product rather than writing a longer workaround.

Quality checklist

  1. Clear symptom, cause and reproducible steps
  2. Versioned articles with review owners
  3. Safe escalation when self-service fails

09 / VANCOUVER COLLABORATION · ORIGINAL GUIDE

A small, measurable pilot for international technology cooperation

A credible cooperation proposal starts with a practical shared outcome rather than unverified claims about offices, contracts or partners. Prospective collaborators need to understand the scope, required inputs, time horizon, deliverables and how success will be measured.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Prepare a short pilot charter covering the client's problem, an initial sample dataset, confidentiality expectations and the features that must work before acceptance. Define ownership of the prototype, the review process and the decision point for expanding or stopping. Adapt language and accessibility to the actual users, not an imagined universal audience.

Illustrative scenario

A Vancouver business evaluating bilingual supplier onboarding could start with a prototype covering ten sample suppliers and a single approval workflow. Success might mean that test users submit accurate information and administrators review it without manual re-entry. Describe the pilot as a proposal until real participation and permissions are verified.

Quality checklist

  1. Bounded deliverables and acceptance criteria
  2. Documented data and intellectual-property terms
  3. Honest distinction between proposals and operations

10 / FARM DECISION SUPPORT · ORIGINAL GUIDE

A farm decision dashboard built from transparent assumptions

A useful agricultural dashboard supports decisions under uncertainty instead of presenting a single precise profit number as inevitable. Prices, yields, water limits, labor availability and input costs change; farmers and managers need to explore how outcomes shift across feasible scenarios.

Read the practical method and example

Implementation method

Start with a well-defined production unit such as one crop, field and season. Record expected yield, farm-gate price, variable costs, water and labor needs in consistent units. Provide a baseline, downside and improvement scenario. Keep calculation methods visible and identify which figures are observed and which are user estimates.

Illustrative scenario

If an illustrative field's expected revenue is 5,000 monetary units and variable costs are 3,200, the contribution margin is 1,800 before fixed costs, financing and taxes. A lower sale price or reduced yield may change the preferred crop. Showing sensitivity to those assumptions is more useful than labeling 1,800 as guaranteed profit.

Quality checklist

  1. Traceable inputs and consistent units
  2. Alternative scenarios and downside sensitivity
  3. Clear distinction between margin and net profit
