## C85 — UNE abstract utility numerator gate (new source-level adjudication)

The [University of New England institutional abstract](https://researchers.une.edu.au/en/publications/using-a-mobile-phone-short-messaging-service-sms-for-irrigation-s-5/) reports 72 irrigators, 45 full-season reporters, 13 partial reporters, 14 non-reporters, and six of the 13 partial reporters who later regretted stopping. Its **80% overall usefulness** statement cannot be uniquely reconstructed from these facts: `(45+6)/72 = 70.83%`; `(45+13)/72 = 80.56%` only if all partial reporters were counted as finding the system useful, which the abstract does not establish. Neither calculation is an authenticated item-response numerator. This is **a new uncertainty about the source's inferential definition**, not proof of a numerical typo or a replacement estimate. The 80% remains an author-reported usefulness appraisal, not event adherence, observed adoption or a verified survey prevalence. ## C86 — adjudicated absence and source-frame corrections (substantive)

**Prospective event classification corrected:** An eligible issued actionable recommendation with independently verified receipt and **adequate continuous observation of no prescribed action during its prespecified window** is a verified departure **D**, not an unknown **U**. If the action log is absent, the sensor failed, the diary is missing, or the observation window is insufficient, code **U**; absence of evidence is not evidence of no irrigation. **B** remains definitively verified nonreceipt, not an action departure. All categories remain disjoint, with `N=I+D+B+U`. The secondary `I/(I+D)` must be named **receipt-and-action-resolved conditional fraction**, not “complete-adjudication fraction,” because B is resolved but excluded from this receipt-conditional denominator. These are proposed methodological definitions, not validated field estimates.

**Source-frame audit:** The Car author-uploaded Table 5's “Never sent” count **17** is inconsistent with the University of New England institutional abstract's **14** never-reporters; this is additional to Table 5's 74-versus-72 total and the Table 6 41-versus-40 respondent conflict. No corrected count is inferred. In Cvejić, the 2016 **35 farms expressing interest** and the abstract's **35 implementation farms** are not independently authenticated as an identical signed/enrolled/responding cohort. Preserve these distinctions in the main eight-case matrix. Neither source discrepancy establishes misconduct, causal effects or compliance.

**Author decision:** Approve or revise the definitions and frame restrictions only after inspecting the primary sources. If the author chooses a different adherence estimand (e.g. physical agreement irrespective of receipt), rewrite the B structural-nonreceipt bound before publication. Full field validation remains unperformed.

Engagement, perceived usefulness, reported behavior change and independently observed irrigation implementation remain distinct constructs.

## C82 actionable-advice adjudication — author-ready correction

**New source finding, not a new experiment.** In the [Car et al. author-uploaded article, p. 139 §3.4 and p. 142 §4.2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257018309_Using_a_mobile_phone_Short_Messaging_Service_SMS_for_irrigation_scheduling_in_Australia_-_Farmers%27_participation_and_utility_evaluation), **25%** of respondents describe accumulating suggested run-time minutes across several days, and **79%** describe combining the DSS with other information sources; the inspected text does **not authenticate item-specific response denominators**. The authors also say their DSS did not dictate when or how much water to apply. Thus a message containing advisory run-time information cannot automatically be treated as a prescriptive event with a binary adherence outcome. This is distinct from the already documented Table 5/6 contradictions.

**Proposed final-text paragraph for substantive human-author review:**

> For Car et al. (2012), the reported SMS dripper-run-time guidance was not an obligatory event-specific irrigation prescription. The authors describe discretionary irrigation timing and amount, and respondents report accumulating suggested run-time information over several days (25%) or combining it with other decision inputs (79%); the item-level denominators for these percentages were not independently authenticated. Accordingly, neither these reported practices nor the 53/72 decision-consideration measure defines an independently observed advice-event adherence rate. Any future G/D/R/A audit must first distinguish prescriptive, conditional and informational messages and predefine an actionable target and tolerance; otherwise adherence is not defined rather than zero.

**Author decision required:** Accept this conservative distinction and the C76 v10 actionable-event denominator, or remove all Car percentages with unverified item Ns. Do not assign a binary implementation rate retrospectively. Original publisher pages, raw message texts and respondent ledgers remain required for stronger quantitative inference. This correction improves conceptual validity but does not resolve the 40/41, 74/72 or Amini/Cvejić conflicts.

**Author-ready passage for possible adoption:**

> The source-level comparisons of Lorite et al. (2012) and Baroni et al. (2019) demonstrate that agreement between recommended and applied *seasonal water volumes*, agreement among irrigation models, and verified implementation of *individual actionable advice events* are different estimands. Lorite's reported annual relative irrigation-supply values (approximately 0.23–0.94 across crops) are ratios of seasonal volumes, not event-compliance percentages. Baroni's five-of-six agreement concerns six scheduling methods, not five of six growers following advice, and its rare-implementation conclusion is qualitative in the accessible publisher abstract. These reports therefore establish important prior art but do not supply an authenticated recommendation-delivery-receipt-action ledger for the proposed G/D/R/A protocol. Full original methods are needed for stronger behavioral or causal claims.

**Gate:** Accept or revise the three-estimand separation in C76 v10 §5.4. Do not recode ARIS or five-of-six as G/D/R/A, and do not present this abstract-based adjudication as a completed full-methods review. No new DOI, human peer-review or author sign-off is claimed.
