## Material correction already incorporated in the manuscript

The earlier C76 v2 manuscript **overstated three Car (2012) numerical contradictions** by testing the 46%, 73% and 80% *influence* claims exclusively against the 72-person *Table 5* denominator. The subsequent C78 pack overcorrected by treating an alternative numerical match as proof of a Table 6 source crosswalk. Inspection of the [author-uploaded printed text, pp. 139–141](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257018309_Using_a_mobile_phone_Short_Messaging_Service_SMS_for_irrigation_scheduling_in_Australia_-_Farmers%27_participation_and_utility_evaluation) shows that the **five unnumbered behavior categories of Table 6** can reproduce the percentages arithmetically, **without establishing that they are the categories referenced by the source's numbered-group prose**:

| Behavior class in printed Table 6 | Count | Evidence meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Small | 11 | Reported small influence / reassurance |
| Large | 10 | Reported large change |
| Significant | 9 | Reported influence with change magnitude unclear |
| None | 8 | Reported no change |
| Marginal | 3 | Reported educational influence without changed action |
| **Total printed Table 6** | **41** | **Printed preceding prose says 40 respondents — unresolved** |

**C81 source-internal semantic crosswalk, not a publisher correction:** The interview discussion immediately after Table 6 describes Groups **1** (confidence/little change), **2** (applied more on trust), **3** (change magnitude not articulable), **4** (rejected excessive advice) and **5** (educational interest without action change). These correspond in order to Table 6 **Small=11, Large=10, Significant=9, None=8, Marginal=3**. This independent semantic agreement plus (Large+Significant)/41=**46.34%**, plus Small=**73.17%**, plus Marginal=**80.49%** strongly supports an **internally reconstructed** Table 6 reading of the authors' 46/73/80 influence sequence. The five categories remain unnumbered in the printed table and the source says **40 respondents** in prose but tabulates **41**; no authenticated Elsevier version/erratum or raw item ledger exists here. Thus these are **author-reported perceived influence rates with an uncertain true denominator**, not independently verified prevalence, event-level implementation or measured savings. The separate Table 5 74/72 and label contradictions remain open.

**Still unresolved:** Table 5 counts 26+7+3+6+15+17 = **74** against printed 72; printed percentages sum **103.2%**; Group 4/5 prose-versus-table labels are reversed; first three Table 5 counts 36/72 = 50% versus prose 68%; Table 6 printed 41 versus preceding prose 40; Table 5 “never sent” 17 versus [UNE institutional abstract](https://rune.une.edu.au/entities/publication/68f9d1ef-6e62-415a-b831-0beb96df9853) 14. **No invented corrected counts, causal irrigation-savings inference or recommendation-event adherence rate.**

## Author-ready corrected paragraph (may be incorporated into final manuscript)

> The Car et al. (2012) author-uploaded report includes a 72-person water-balance grouping (Table 5) and an unnumbered 41-response behavior-change grouping (Table 6), although the accompanying prose states 40 respondents. The reported 46%, 73%, and 80% influence sequence can be reconstructed from Table 6 categories (19/41, 30/41, 33/41); moreover, the adjacent five numbered interview-group descriptions align semantically with Small, Large, Significant, None and Marginal in that order. This supports a source-internal interpretation of the authors' reported influence but cannot establish the true respondent denominator, because the prose says 40 and Table 6 totals 41, or validate objectively observed adherence. Table 5's six counts total 74 against a printed 72, with conflicting group labels. The same printed text reports that 53/72 irrigators said they actively considered DSS information when scheduling, while the UNE abstract reports 45/72 returned data all season; these are distinct, overlapping-unknown outcomes and neither verifies event-level implementation. The paper also reports 69% wished to use it the next season, an intention rather than confirmed subsequent use. Engagement, perceived usefulness, reported behavior change and independently observed irrigation implementation remain distinct constructs.
