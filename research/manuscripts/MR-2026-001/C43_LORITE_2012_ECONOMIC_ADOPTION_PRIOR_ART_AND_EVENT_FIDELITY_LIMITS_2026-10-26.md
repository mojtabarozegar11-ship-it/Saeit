# MR-2026-001 C43 — Lorite et al. (2012): original economic adoption and seasonal recommendation-practice comparator

**26 October 2026 | Primary published abstract and bibliographic audit only | MAJOR REVISION / NO SUBMISSION**

## Identified source

Lorite, I. J., García-Vila, M., Carmona, M.-A., Santos, C. & Soriano, M.-A. (2012). *Assessment of the Irrigation Advisory Services' Recommendations and Farmers' Irrigation Management: A Case Study in Southern Spain*. **Water Resources Management 26**, 2397–2419. DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s11269-012-0023-3 . Public bibliographic/abstract record: https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/waterr/v26y2012i8p2397-2419.html . The **full publisher Methods were not accessed** in this audit; no farmer-level logs, tables or unreported sample size can be inferred.

## Source-reported design and measures

- Evaluated **five years** of Local Irrigation Advisory Services (LIAS) in the Genil–Cabra Irrigation Scheme in southern Spain, comparing actual irrigation with LIAS recommendations.
- Authors describe **ARIS LIAS** as actual irrigation applied divided by the advisory recommendation; the abstract reports approximately **0.23** for wheat and sunflower and **0.94** for maize, with marked farmer variability. This is **seasonal aggregate adherence/acceptance evidence**, not merely an algorithm accuracy measure.
- Crucially, the same published abstract also evaluates **Irrigation Water Productivity (IWP)** and **Irrigation Water Benefit (IWB)**, so economics and farmer acceptance are **already jointly addressed by prior art**.
- Reported **IWP** under optimal irrigation schedules: approximately **€0.23/m³** for wheat/sunflower/maize, **€0.53/m³** for cotton/sugar beet, and **>€2/m³** for garlic. Reported **IWB** examples: wheat **€0.06/m³**, sunflower **€0.13/m³**. These are author-reported crop-level figures from the abstract, not newly estimated economic effects or transferable 2026 prices.

## New editorial implications for MR

1. A claim that this manuscript is the first to compare advice with actual irrigation, to quantify adoption, or to connect adherence with irrigation profitability is **contradicted by the Lorite 2012 abstract**.
2. ARIS LIAS is a **ratio of aggregated applied and recommended water**. It does not, from its definition alone, uniquely identify recommendation-specific event count, event dates or event-specific dose. This is an **identification limitation of the measure**, not evidence that the original authors made a calculation error.
3. A possible *narrower* question for MR is whether an independently source-linked **event-level** G/D/R/A dataset adds information over seasonal ARIS **and** whether it predicts farmer adoption or economic return after controlling for baseline farm factors. No such dataset or causal evaluation has yet been obtained.
4. Do **not** treat reported IWP/IWB as independent new data; they are published predecessor results. Do **not** assert the full Lorite Methods have been checked or infer that event-level timestamps were absent from their raw study.
5. Author review should explicitly distinguish **seasonal adherence, event fidelity, measured water use, yield and profit** as separate constructs, and avoid novelty claims based on their mere co-occurrence.

## Decision and next empirical gate

The newly documented economic comparator makes the MR originality hurdle **more demanding**, not less. Full-text methods, recommendation/event logs, farmer-level denominators and actual original-source event matching remain unavailable.
