## Proposed final Section 2 methodological text

Eight primary studies were **purposively selected** to contrast evidence types rather than estimate a pooled effect or adoption prevalence: Amini (participatory within-farm comparison), Cvejić (advisory programme and reported uptake), Jamal (field observations and virtual decisions), Abi Saab (experimental plots), Car (SMS advice and user records), Elia (commercial paired-farm plots), Baroni (user survey and implementation gap), and Lorite (actual-to-recommended irrigation ratios). Inclusion required an identifiable original publication, a relevant farmer-facing irrigation advice or scheduling question, and at least one source-anchored methodological or outcome statement. Selection was based on contrasts in evidence provenance, unit, time horizon and design, **not** on an exhaustive search, a statistically representative sample or favorable results. Where full methods were not accessible (notably Elia, Baroni and Lorite), inferences were restricted to statements supported by publisher abstracts/previews. Bibliographic discovery totals describe search operations only and are not PRISMA eligibility counts. No dual-human screening, systematic search, meta-analysis or prevalence estimate was performed.

For each numerical or behavioral statement, the extraction unit was the **claim**, not the entire article. The audit records source locator, denominator (person, farm, field, plot, advice event or simulated season), exposure, implementation provenance, measured or modeled endpoint, comparator, uncertainty and post-support follow-up. **M** denotes physically measured field outcomes but does not automatically imply a causal estimate. **O** denotes objectively verifiable human action on a particular recommendation. **S** denotes self-report, perceptions or user-entered operational records. **P** denotes predictions, simulations or hypothetical scenarios. **U** denotes absent, incompatible or unverified provenance. Categories can coexist for different claims within one publication. Message receipt or self-entered irrigation is not independent proof of the applied water volume; lack of an O record means **unverified adherence**, not proven nonadherence.

A water-saving percentage was classified as observed only when a physical field-water endpoint and comparator were described; field measurement alone does not establish randomization or causal attribution. Conditional model savings remain P even when measured weather or soil data inform the simulation. Reported use remains S even if another endpoint in the same article has M evidence. No denominator was imputed across 35 initial Slovenian farms, 46 later advice recipients and the 83% farmer-use statement; these refer to potentially different units or periods. In Amini, the published F7 water-productivity figures are inconsistent (Table 4: 4.6 versus 6.0 kg/m³, treatment-minus-control −1.4; conclusion: −0.1); the discrepancy is retained as U, not silently reconciled. Car's machine-readable Table 5 counts were not promoted to precise event denominators without visual verification. The approach is a transparent **editorial claim-to-evidence audit**, not a validated adherence instrument or first-ever analysis of the advice-to-action gap.
