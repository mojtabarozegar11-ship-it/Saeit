# MR-2026-001 — Construct boundary audit 06: Hrozencik et al.
**Status:** Targeted lead, **not** a systematically screened record. Date 2026-10-24.
Source: Hrozencik RA, Suter JF, Ferraro PJ, Hendricks N (2024). *Social comparisons and groundwater use: Evidence from Colorado and Kansas*. American Journal of Agricultural Economics 106(2):946–966. DOI [10.1111/ajae.12415](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajae.12415). [Publisher abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ajae.12415).

## What the publisher abstract establishes
- Preregistered randomized field intervention, **over 3,000 irrigators**, Colorado and Kansas, USA; annual groundwater-use peer comparisons versus no comparison.
- Publisher-reported average annual groundwater reduction **4.05%**, 95% CI **−5.87% to −2.21%** (signed reduction convention), over a three-year intervention; within-irrigator spillovers also reported.
- These are **the authors' causal estimates**, not results of MR-2026-001. Full-text verification of outcome metering, randomization unit, missingness, compliance, and registration remains outstanding.

## Why this is a negative control
The intervention delivers social-norm information about groundwater consumption; the abstract does **not** establish crop-specific irrigation timing/depth/amount advice from an irrigation decision-support system. It should not be mislabeled an irrigation DSS simply because it changes water behavior. In a strict farmer-facing DSS review, provisional status is **contextual/excluded at full-text confirmation**; in a broader behavior-intervention review it might qualify, but that is a different question.

## Methodological use
A genuine randomized field study can support causal water-use claims without supporting the distinct construct of **DSS adoption**. Preserve outcome validity and intervention construct as independent axes. No adoption of a computerized irrigation DSS is claimed. **Do not count this lead in PRISMA.**
