## Direct source-to-claim reconciliation
1. **Design/denominator:** IrrigaSys reportedly supported ~30 farmers/year, ~103 plots/year and ~2,080 ha over 2017–2022. **Twenty maize center-pivot plots** were purposively selected for the post-evaluation, and farmer schedule-based alternatives were compared with model-optimized alternatives in **2019 and 2022** (Methods 2.3, PDF p. 4–5). Neither the 30 farmers nor 103 plots should be misrepresented as independent analysis units of the 20-plot comparison.
2. **Abstract-versus-body discrepancy:** PDF abstract says **2019 and 2020**; Methods 2.3, Figures 4 and 5, Table 2 and Results repeatedly specify **2019 and 2022**. Treat **2022** as the *body-described comparison year*, explicitly flag the contradiction and do not silently assert that the publisher has corrected its abstract. A corresponding-author clarification would be needed for definitive correction.
3. **Exposure/implementation:** The system generated weekly irrigation schedules. Farmer-reported daily/weekly applied irrigation was obtained through local water-board technicians' weekly surveys; when farmer data were delayed, the system sometimes **assumed adherence** until later updates (Methods 2.3). This is not independently calibrated event-level metering or a verified timestamp-matched recommendation-to-action series.
4. **Outcome measurement:** Seasonal irrigation amounts from farmer schedules were compared to optimized schedules. **Both farmer-schedule and optimized-schedule ETa and yields were simulated with MOHID-Land** (Methods 2.3, PDF p. 5). Thus modeled yields, productivity and multicriteria utility should not be called directly observed crop yields or realized causal water savings.
5. **Recomputed descriptive water-volume contrasts from rounded published means:** Results report 2019 farmer versus model mean irrigation **7,800 vs 8,650 m³/ha**, so (7,800−8,650)/8,650 ×100 = **−9.8266%**. In 2022 **7,940 vs 7,800 m³/ha**, so (7,940−7,800)/7,800 ×100 = **+1.7949%**. These are approximate *published-mean comparisons*, **not event-level adherence percentages, causal savings or independently observed effects**. No inferential standard errors were extracted.
6. **Authors' explicit limitation:** Conclusion says strict farmer adherence **cannot be directly concluded** from their multicriteria results. It also notes that farmer irrigation data collection through surveys was labor-intensive and needed replacement/improvement (PDF pp. 11–12).

## Construct-level novelty impact on MR
This is **direct 2024 prior art explicitly framed around farmer adherence to irrigation DSS advice**, with a 20-plot farmer-versus-optimized-schedule comparison and an environmental/economic multi-criteria framework. MR **cannot claim to be first to evaluate irrigation-DSS farmer adherence or first to distinguish farmers' behavior from model recommendations**. Any incremental contribution must be narrower: transparent event-specific recommendation–receipt–implementation–metered outcome–counterfactual–post-support evidence provenance, empirically validated against prior frameworks. Such validation has **not** occurred.

The study is *not* a randomized adherence experiment; no event-matched independent flow-meter series, inter-rater validation of G/D/R/A or causal effect is demonstrated by this audit. The 20 plots are not 20 randomized independent farmer decisions. A descriptive comparison between actual schedules and model-optimal schedules does not identify causal impacts of receiving advice.

## AI adversarial verdict and author gate
**Substantive prior-art overlap identified; tighten MR novelty claims.** Correct manuscript body and bibliography, retain 2019/2020 versus 2019/2022 source inconsistency explicitly, preserve the mixed observed/reported/modelled measurement labels, and do not fabricate DOI, farmer compliance, water savings or causal conclusions.
