# MR-2026-001 C49 — randomized smart-meter feedback as an adjacent causal evidence comparator

**27 October 2026. Primary publisher abstract audit, not full-method replication.**

## Source
Chabé-Ferret S, Le Coent P, Reynaud A, Subervie J, Lepercq D (2019). *Can we nudge farmers into saving water? Evidence from a randomised experiment.* European Review of Agricultural Economics 46(3):393–416, published 4 June 2019. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/erae/jbz022 ; Oxford Academic publisher abstract: https://academic.oup.com/erae/article-abstract/46/3/393/5510773 (retrieved 27 October 2026).

## What the accessible original publisher abstract actually establishes
- A randomized controlled trial among **200 farmers equipped with irrigation smart meters** in southwestern France.
- Treated farmers received **weekly individual and group water-consumption feedback for four months**.
- The abstract states that medium-to-large overall effects of the nudge could be ruled out; it suggests reductions among high-water users and a possible reduction in the fraction of zero-consumption farmers.
- The **abstract alone does not disclose** the exact allocation, attrition, effect estimates, confidence intervals, clustering or instrument calibration needed to reproduce or independently verify those findings.

## Construct-level relevance to MR
This is **feedback and social-comparison nudging**, not a direct trial of event-specific irrigation DSS recommendations. It nevertheless demonstrates pre-existing **randomized, smart-meter-informed counterfactual evaluation** in agricultural water behavior. It is stronger causal design evidence than self-reported platform engagement, but it does not automatically measure the fraction of particular irrigation advice events acted upon.

| Claim | Source-supported evidence | Unsupported extrapolation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smart-meter feedback can be randomized | 200-farmer randomized design described in publisher abstract | Independent audit of assignment or metering |
| Overall consumption effect | Authors report no medium/large overall nudge effect | Invented numerical treatment effect or universal no-effect claim |
| Heterogeneity | Authors suggest effects differ for high users and zero users | Confirmed subgroup causality without full method and multiplicity controls |
| Event-level DSS fidelity | No such event-linked measure is reported in the accessible abstract | Claim that randomized feedback equals adherence to plot-specific DSS recommendations |

## Adversarial novelty verdict
MR cannot claim to be the first to propose causal irrigation-behavior evaluation, smart-meter feedback, or separating reported engagement from measured water use. Its proposed G/D/R/A worksheet remains an **unvalidated reporting aid**. The new source is **contextual prior art**, not part of the original purposive eight-study set. The primary full-text methods and effect estimates must be examined before citing numerical causal results beyond the abstract. No farm observations, G/D/R/A validation or author approval were created. **MAJOR REVISION / NO SUBMISSION.**
