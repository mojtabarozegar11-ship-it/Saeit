## Source conflict and non-identification
- The institutional abstract partitions all 72 into **45 + 13 + 14 = 72** (all-season/half-season/never-returned data). The author-uploaded Table 5 displays **26 + 7 + 3 + 6 + 15 + 17 = 74** against printed total 72. Its six displayed percentages sum to **103.2%**. The table's 17 never-sent differs from the abstract's 14; the 15 stopped differs from 13 half-season, but category definitions may also differ. No unique repair can be inferred.
- Full-text Results 3.4 states **53/72 actively considered advice**, close to an asserted **75–76% active usage** from input messages. These are **different measurement channels**; numerical closeness is not identity, agreement validation or independently logged irrigation-event compliance.
- The full text says the first three Table 5 groups are **68%**; the printed group counts imply **50%** if divided by all 72. No defensible correction can be selected without source page-image verification or an author correction.
- The 80% utility statement in the institutional abstract is author interpretation, not equal to 58/72 or 53/72 by definition. It must not be reused as a water-saving or advice-compliance effect.
- **No hydrologic causal effect** or sustained post-trial adoption can be identified from these counts.

## Manuscript-ready replacement (source-attributed)
“In Car et al. (2012), 45/72 irrigators returned input data throughout the season, whereas 53/72 reported actively considering the advisory information. These are distinct constructs: data-return engagement and self-reported consideration, neither of which measures independently verified recommendation-event compliance. The author-uploaded Table 5 transcription and the narrative contain conflicting category counts, percentages and a separate 40-versus-41 open-question denominator; until verified against the publisher's original page images, we do not infer adherence rates from these tables.”
