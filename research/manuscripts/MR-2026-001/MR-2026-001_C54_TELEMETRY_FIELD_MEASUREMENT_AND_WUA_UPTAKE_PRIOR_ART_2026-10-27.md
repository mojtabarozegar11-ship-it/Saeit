# MR-2026-001 C54 — Field telemetry versus advice fidelity: two further original-study comparators

**27 October 2026. Internal AI adversarial prior-art screen, not author-approved, peer-reviewed or submitted.** Evidence is from identifiable publisher-indexed abstracts/metadata, not complete publisher Methods or tables. Do not claim full-text verification.

## Comparator A: measured field-level irrigation
Lorite, I. J., Santos, C., García-Vila, M., Carmona, M. A., and Fereres, E. (2013). *Assessing irrigation scheme water use and farmers' performance using wireless telemetry systems*. **Computers and Electronics in Agriculture, 98**, 193–204. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2013.08.007 . Author-uploaded/indexed abstract: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262327450_Assessing_irrigation_scheme_water_use_and_farmers%27_performance_using_wireless_telemetry_systems ; bibliographic metadata: https://client.eurekamag.com/research/064/284/064284018.php .

The abstract describes wireless-telemetry records of irrigation at **1,365 plots**, approximately **7,500 hectares**, during **two irrigation seasons** in the Genil–Cabra scheme in Spain. This is already prior art for actual plot-level water-use monitoring and variability analysis. **It is not established from the abstract** that every plot had a time-stamped, independently documented *individual advisory recommendation*, its delivery, its receipt and its action outcome. Thus, the paper challenges any claim that measured plot irrigation or telemetry-based farmer-performance comparisons are new; it does not, on the currently verified evidence, establish a complete advice-delivery-to-action linkage.

## Comparator B: actual usage, satisfaction and infrastructure functionality
Playán, E., Salvador, R., Bonet, L., et al. (2018). *Assessing telemetry and remote control systems for water users associations in Spain*. **Agricultural Water Management, 202**, 89–98. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agwat.2018.02.015 . Indexed publisher abstract: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/agiwat/v202y2018icp89-98.html .

The study reports a **110-question survey of 84 water-user associations** and **24 stakeholder interviews**. Its abstract reports dissatisfaction in **15% of surveyed WUAs** and inability to operate half of connected hydraulic valves in **19% of surveyed WUAs**. These are association-level reports; they must not be re-labelled farmer-level adherence percentages, objective field savings or causal effects. The reported operational limitation is a useful example of why installation, operability, use and actual water outcome require separate evidence.

## Revised novelty boundary
1. Lorite (2012) already compares annual farmer-applied irrigation with advisory recommendations (ARIS_LIAS). Lorite (2013) already observes field-level applied irrigation by telemetry. Playán (2018) already investigates infrastructure use and operational constraints.
2. Therefore, **neither advice-versus-applied irrigation, nor telemetry, nor adoption/operability distinctions are original discoveries of MR**. A prospective incremental contribution might be a reproducible **event-level** evidence-provenance audit of recommendation generation, delivery, receipt, action, meter verification and outcomes (G/D/R/A), with consistent denominators and uncertainty.
3. That contribution remains **hypothetical** until complete full texts and a real source-by-source overlap audit show the proposed linkage adds explanatory value beyond these studies and the existing review literature. No empirical G/D/R/A validation or new field experiment exists.
4. Do not add either 2013 or 2018 study to the eight-case **included primary study** count without actual author-defined eligibility and original-method verification. They are presently *adjacent novelty comparators*, not newly screened included studies.
