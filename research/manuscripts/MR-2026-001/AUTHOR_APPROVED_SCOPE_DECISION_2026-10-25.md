## Approved review question and PCC

- **Population (P):** farmers and farm managers making irrigation decisions, including managers of commercial farms and field trials, with the status of actual farmer exposure recorded separately from the intended target user.
- **Concept (C):** farmer-facing irrigation decision-support systems that provide an actionable irrigation recommendation (when, how much, frequency, scheduling, or allocation) to a human decision-maker; differentiate recommendation generation, delivery, access, acceptance, implementation and sustained use.
- **Context (C):** real agricultural production/field settings under water-scarcity or water-management constraints. Document region, crop, farm context, comparator, season, and follow-up. Water scarcity must be operationally defined from each paper or recorded as unreported; do not infer it from country alone.

**Primary research stratum A (core synthesis):** evaluations or adoption studies of farmer-/farm-manager-facing systems delivering actionable irrigation recommendations in real farm/production contexts. A field evaluation with no published adoption measurement can enter A if the human-facing recommendation and the eligible field context are evidenced; adoption then reads *not reported*, not *zero* or *positive*. An adoption evaluation must distinguish stated intention, self-report, observed implementation and independent long-term reuse.

**Secondary contextual stratum B (fully autonomous controllers):** closed-loop irrigation controllers that operate without a documented farmer-facing decision recommendation. Describe technical/field performance separately, never combine with A's farmer-adoption outcomes, and do not treat autonomous actuation as farmer adoption.

**Secondary contextual stratum C (model-only / virtual):** simulated irrigation regimes, counterfactual optimized schedules, virtual farmer exercises and validation using historical inputs without verified post-deployment farm application. Describe prediction/validation separately; do not claim realized savings, field effectiveness or actual adoption. A study may be tagged both B and C (separate attributes), but count unique study records once.

**Other contextual/excluded cases:** passive sensors, monitoring dashboards, evapotranspiration estimates or yield models without evidence of an actionable recommendation to a farmer are not core A. Where secondary context is relevant, give an explicit reason and never inflate primary included-study counts.

## Mandatory evidence coding (prospective)

For every candidate source record: source/DOI if verified, retrieval and access level (abstract, full text, table), design, participants/sites, unit of analysis, crop/region/season, recommendation mechanism/recipient, empirical implementation, comparator, observation interval, measured irrigation and yield, farmer adoption stage, uncertainty, attrition, conflicts/funding and author-noted limitations. Tag each result by provenance: **M** directly measured, **O** observed human irrigation action, **S** self-report, **P** prediction/simulation/counterfactual, **U** unverified/unclear. Mixed evidence must preserve multiple tags rather than converting P to M.

## Prespecified distinctions and reporting

1. A system's *field-specific output* is not the same as field-level validation if benchmarked only against aggregated basin/annual withdrawal.
2. Farmer exposure, intention, satisfaction, stated use, observed uptake, and multi-season voluntary use are distinct outcomes.
3. Percent deviations between predicted and actual irrigation plans are not automatically realized water savings or causal treatment effects.
4. Null findings and adverse agronomic tradeoffs remain eligible; do not select only statistically significant results.
5. No quantitative pooled effect or PRISMA flow is to be generated until searches, de-duplication, genuine screening and source extraction justify it.
6. Maintain a dated change log for later protocol modifications; preserve the original version and state deviations transparently.
