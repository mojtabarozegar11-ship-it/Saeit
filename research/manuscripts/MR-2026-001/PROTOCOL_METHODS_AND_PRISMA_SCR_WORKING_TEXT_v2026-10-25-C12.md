## Original English methods draft (not yet executed)

### Objective and eligibility
This scoping review will map how farmer-facing irrigation decision-support systems (DSS) have been evaluated in water-constrained agricultural settings, with emphasis on the provenance of water-use outcomes and the difference between availability of a recommendation and its adoption in practice. We will use the Population–Concept–Context framework to specify eligibility. The population comprises farmers or farm managers responsible for irrigation decisions, including those participating in field evaluations. The primary concept is a system that produces an actionable irrigation recommendation—timing, volume, frequency, scheduling, or field allocation—communicated to a human operator. The context is real agricultural production under water scarcity, constrained allocation, or a clearly described water-management challenge. The term 'scarcity' will not be inferred solely from geographic location; authors' descriptions of rainfall, water allocations, irrigation restrictions, water deficit, or analogous contextual measures will be recorded.

Primary-stratum studies (A) must demonstrate a farmer-facing actionable recommendation and relevant real-farm exposure or assessment. Field studies without adoption measures remain eligible for the field-validation question, but their adoption status will be coded 'not reported'. Fully autonomous closed-loop controllers without documented operator recommendations will be mapped in a distinct contextual stratum (B), without attributing autonomous actuation to farmer adoption. Studies limited to simulation, optimized counterfactual irrigation schedules, virtual choice workshops, or model-to-model comparison without verified field implementation will be assigned to a separate contextual stratum (C). Studies can carry multiple descriptive tags but will be counted once at the publication/study level. Passive monitoring systems without actionable farmer-facing recommendations will not be counted as primary-stratum evidence.

### Information sources and search strategy — prospective
Database-specific strategies will be developed for at least two accessible scholarly indexes with agricultural and engineering coverage (candidate sources include Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, CAB Abstracts and AGRICOLA), supplemented by documented citation chaining and targeted public discovery. No database is claimed as searched until a complete executed query, timestamp, search interface, result count and raw export are archived. The working conceptual search blocks are (irrigat* OR water management) AND ('decision support' OR 'irrigation scheduling' OR 'recommendation system') AND (farmer* OR farm management OR adoption OR field trial OR on-farm) AND (scarcity OR drought OR water deficit OR water saving). Syntax, field restrictions, spelling variants, proximity operators and database date ranges will be tailored and frozen before formal screening. These terms are **illustrative**, not a record of executed index searches. Searches will be documented in full with exact strings, dates, interfaces, filters, export filenames, hashes and retrieval counts.

### Selection and reproducibility — prospective
Records will be imported into a preserved bibliographic register retaining database-specific IDs, source URLs, DOIs and the original source of each record. Automated exact-match deduplication will use normalized DOI where available, followed by bibliographic title/year/author checks and human adjudication of ambiguous matches. Screening will be carried out by identified qualified human reviewers against a versioned eligibility guide; disagreements and the adjudication procedure will be logged. If a second independent reviewer cannot be secured, this will be explicitly disclosed and no independent dual-screening claim will be made. Full-text exclusions will have study-specific reasons. PRISMA-ScR counts will be generated **only from real exports and logged decisions**, never reconstructed from the current pilot discovery set.

### Data charting and outcome classification — prospective
Each source will be charted by verified bibliographic identifier and document access level, country and crop, number of farms and farmers, study duration, irrigation technology, recommendation delivery modality, human decision authority, water-scarcity definition, intervention and comparator, allocation mechanism, irrigation flow measurement, crop yield measurement, attrition and follow-up. Outcomes will be recorded with unit and direction, uncertainty and reported statistical model. We will separately tag direct field measurement (M), observed implementation of farmer action (O), self-reported attitudes or use (S), simulated/predicted or counterfactual outcomes (P), and unverifiable claims (U). A result can carry multiple provenance tags when components of the same paper differ. Neither simulation-based water savings nor model agreement with another model will be relabeled as field-verified realized savings.

Adoption will be coded on distinct observable dimensions: system access and exposure, stated intention, perceived usefulness or satisfaction, self-reported use, observed execution of specific recommendations, repeat use, and sustained voluntary use after active trial support. The denominator will be the relevant farmer, farm, plot, decision event or time period, not whichever sample size is largest in the publication. A paper with only participant workshops cannot automatically be assigned an adoption rate. Where authors report pooled savings across multiple irrigation systems, we will retain irrigation type and crop-specific estimates to prevent inappropriate aggregation.

### Critical appraisal and synthesis — prospective
Although formal risk-of-bias scoring is not mandatory for a scoping review, the chart will identify the allocation mechanism, comparability of control and treatment plots, weather and crop confounding, seasonality, measurement calibration, participant recruitment, adherence documentation, missingness, outcome assessor independence and external validity. Studies reporting both beneficial and adverse outcomes will be charted. Descriptive maps will summarize study design, evidence provenance and observed stages of adoption by intervention type. Quantitative pooling will not be attempted unless comparable outcomes and defensible denominators emerge and a separate, transparently documented analytical plan is approved. Any departures from the prespecified eligibility criteria will be dated and justified.

### Additional candidate source: human adoption contrasted with scenario modeling
Cvejić et al. (2020), *Agronomy* 10(9), 1238, DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy10091238, reports a multi-year farmer-facing irrigation recommendation initiative in the Vipava Valley (35 farms originally expressed interest). In 2020, **83% of responding farmers reported using the system**, while **91% had earlier failed to regularly complete irrigation diaries**. Thus these uptake and behavior categories have important self-report / missingness qualifications and should not be treated as independent usage telemetry. The paper's prominent **25% potential reduction in irrigation volume and 24% reduction in energy and CO₂ are conditional simulations**, not empirically demonstrated post-implementation resource savings. The official HTML also contains an unresolved statement referring to "Forty-six farmers" in the context of 35 initial farms; do not convert this to "46%" without a publisher correction or denominator clarification. Source anchoring and caution notes: `PRIMARY_SOURCE_CVEJIC_2020_FIELD_ADOPTION_PROVENANCE_2026-10-25.md`. **Candidate only; never counted as formally included without human screening.**
