## Newly verified primary field study
Amini, A.; Emami, S.; Dehghanisanij, H. (2025). *Participatory evaluation of an irrigation decision support system for water-saving and productivity gains in Lake Urmia Basin*. **Scientific Reports** 15, article 42480. Published 27 November 2025. DOI **10.1038/s41598-025-26567-z**. Official publisher: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-26567-z. This is the verified publisher DOI, not an invented identifier.

### Study design and intervention (publisher sections)
- **Materials and methods > Study area / Characteristics of selected farms**: 2021–2022 crop year, Mahabad Plain, Lake Urmia basin, Iran; 80 farms/orchards initially selected from four villages, eight monitoring farms/orchards chosen (two per village). Within each of eight farms, treatment and customary-management control sections were compared. These are **within-farm comparisons, not evidence of randomized allocation**.
- **Decision-support system**: online weather, soil, crop, water-availability and irrigation-system inputs; operator-facing recommendations via web and mobile interfaces; farmer can enter irrigation events and receive updated irrigation schedules. **Primary stratum A candidate**: actionable advice reaches a human farmer/operator; the farmer attempted to follow schedules in treatment sections.
- **Methods > Collecting data on water flow**: surface water delivery measured with WSC flumes and hydrograph integration; sprinkler output timed using 20-L container; drip emitter output by graduated container; soil moisture sampled at 0–30 and 30–60 cm; yield assessed by treatment/control at season end. These are described *direct field measurements*, not model predictions, but calibration and replication details require further checking.
- **Methods > Stakeholder participation**: PRA workshops, semi-structured interviews, before/after subjective ratings (1–10) for five perceived outcomes. These are **self-reported stakeholder appraisals** and should never be treated as objectively metered energy/cost/time changes. The text describes 20 workshop participants in a village context and separately eight monitoring farmers in later evaluation; do not assume 20 or 80 independent adopters.
- **Results > Irrigation management / Table 4**: four monitored units (F3, F5, F6, F8) had *reported* treatment-minus-control irrigation-depth reductions of 158.4, 253.6, 309.0 and 41.2 mm respectively; F4 and F7 had higher irrigation depth; F1/F2 wheat fields had no substantive difference. These are study-reported measured comparisons, not our new experiments. A pooled effect across unlike crops/systems is NOT justified.
- **Results > Water productivity and crop yield / Table 5**: reported water-productivity differences for F5 +3.8 kg/m³, F6 +0.15 kg/m³ in one passage, F4 −0.2 kg/m³, F3 +1.3 kg/m³, F7 −1.3 kg/m³, F8 +0.1 kg/m³. The same paper later gives F6 approximately +0.2 and F7 −1.4, while conclusion says F7 −0.1 kg/m³. **These discordant within-article values remain unresolved** pending direct table/PDF validation.
- **Results > Participatory evaluation**: farmer exposure and guided implementation in eight monitoring farms are evidenced; multi-season voluntary continued adoption after study closure is **not established**. Separate training attendance, application use, and independent farmer-initiated sustained adoption.

### Evidence taxonomy and methodological caution
| Claim | Source locator | Evidence label | What it does *not* establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treatment-control irrigation depths | Results / Table 4 discussion; field flow measurement method | M = measured/field reported | No random assignment, independent replication, or causal generalization |
| Crop yield and water productivity | Results / Table 5 discussion; Eq. (3) WP=Y/I | M = measured/field reported, QA pending | No independently audited raw data; table-level inconsistencies |
| Farmer followed treatment recommendations | Results / Irrigation management | O = observed/reported implementation, study-reported | Not retention, diffusion or longitudinal adoption |
| Farmer ratings of savings/time/energy/cost | Methods / Stakeholder participation and PRA workshops | S = self-report | Not direct metered energy or monetary savings |
| Seasonal model-to-CROPWAT agreement (<±7%) | Results / Water productivity discussion | P = model-vs-model comparison | Not independent field prediction accuracy |

**Bias appraisal:** within-farm allocation details unclear; baseline comparability and selection of eight monitoring farms may bias generalization; possible farmer/Hawthorne effects and treatment contamination; outcome assessment not blinded; uncertainty for individual crops/systems not fully established from text; 2021–2022 single-season setting. These are *reviewer concerns*, not confirmed flaws.

### Independent arithmetic and discrepancies (limited to source-stated figures)
- Publisher Methods / Study area: `110 L/s × 3 h × 3600 s/h ÷ 1000 L/m³ = 1188 m³` per hectare per irrigation turn, **matches** reported 1188 m³/ha.
- Publisher Results: among eight monitoring units, four with reductions, two with increases, two essentially unchanged. This is a categorical check based on publisher text, **not a pooled savings estimate**.
- F6 WP difference appears as +0.15 kg/m³ and about +0.2 kg/m³; F7 as −1.3, −1.4 and −0.1 kg/m³ in different passages. Hold numerical synthesis pending actual Table 5 and any correction/erratum.
- Publisher reports a paired t-test at p<0.05, but underlying replicate-level data and assumptions have NOT been independently reanalyzed. Do not claim independent confirmation of significance.
