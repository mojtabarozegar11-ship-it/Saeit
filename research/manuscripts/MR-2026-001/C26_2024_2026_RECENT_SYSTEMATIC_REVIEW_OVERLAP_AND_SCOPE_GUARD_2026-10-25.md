## Three additional source-level checks

**1. Belfiore & Viaggi (2026), full open publisher article.** *Mathematical Programming Models for Agricultural Water: A Systematic Review*, *Water* 18(17), 2174, [DOI 10.3390/w18172174](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/18/17/2174). Published **3 September 2026**. The authors report **42** included studies from **438** initial records. Their research questions concern mathematical programming models, economic/policy instruments, agricultural water allocation and heterogeneous agents. They explicitly observe that only **four** reviewed models represent behavioural differences between agents. This is strong prior art against any claim that the **farmer-behaviour or model–practice gap** is newly identified. However, its primary unit is a **mathematical programming model/policy instrument**, not a source-audited **timestamped advice event linked to a verified irrigation action and causal water outcome**. **Decision:** retain as essential recent contextual prior art; do not merge model-review results with eight primary irrigation-advice case studies or claim it proves the worksheet's originality.

**2. Pronti, Auci & Berbel (2024), verified bibliographic abstract via DOAJ/RePEc.** *Water conservation and saving technologies for irrigation. A structured literature review of econometric studies on the determinants of adoption*, *Agricultural Water Management* 299:108838, [DOI 10.1016/j.agwat.2024.108838](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agwat.2024.108838). Reports **36** studies selected for in-depth analysis from an initial **1,027** records; classifies farm, farmer, institutional and climatic/geographic determinants of irrigation water-saving technology adoption. This directly predates any claim that an adoption literature review or evidence classification is novel. The accessible bibliographic abstract does **not** demonstrate event-specific matching of individual DSS advice to metered actions. **Decision:** add to the novelty challenge; do not attribute uninspected full-text methods to its authors.

**3. Liu et al. (2026), publisher abstract.** *Application of Irrigation Decision-Making Methods for Smart Irrigation Systems: A Review*, *Irrigation and Drainage* 75(3):1250–1264, [DOI 10.1002/ird.70098](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ird.70098), published **19 January 2026**. Reviews algorithmic decision-making and machine-learning smart irrigation, calling for closed-loop systems and more usable interfaces. Its publicly accessible abstract does not establish that it validates independent farmer adherence at the advice-event level. **Decision:** contextual technical prior art, not an event-level empirical validation.

## Editorial implications for the next MR author version

- **Do not** describe the 2026 Belfiore review as a study of individual farmers' response to daily DSS messages: its models concern water management policy and optimization, with behavioral heterogeneity as a limitation.
- **Do not** describe Pronti et al.'s econometric determinants of irrigation technology adoption as an event-level study of implementation of particular recommendations. It nonetheless invalidates generic novelty claims about adoption factors.
- **Do not** claim the six-domain provenance worksheet has been validated by any of these reviews. The incremental distinction remains a **proposed reporting unit and falsifiable evaluation**, not a demonstrated innovation.
- Before preparing a journal-final manuscript, include and accurately cite at least the **2026 Belfiore** and **2024 Pronti** studies in the literature context; retain the access-level caveat for the latter. Human author must judge whether the resulting claim is substantive enough for a Perspective.
- Keep the **MR-2026-001** and any later water-policy mathematical programming article separate by research question and dataset; do not split the same extraction into salami-sliced articles.
