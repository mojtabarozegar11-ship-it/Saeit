# MR-2026-001 — Cycle 11 — evidence and novelty comparison
Two new distinct primary sources checked; neither previously appears among the cycle 5–10 file names.
| Dimension | Bonet et al. 2026 | Bernhardt et al. 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context | Commercial olive orchard, Spain, 2022–23 | Southern Germany region, 2017–23 |
| System output | Weekly mobile irrigation recommendations | Spatial model estimates for planners/policymakers |
| Field measurement | On-farm irrigation volumes and physiological variables reported in abstract | Aggregated annual measured withdrawals at well level |
| Model output | Sensor-based scheduling model | Field-scale predicted demand and scenarios |
| Real-world adoption | Not established from accessible abstract | Not documented |
| Strongest warranted inference | Reported field comparison, not longitudinal adoption | Aggregate prediction validity, not farm-level treatment efficacy |
| Current eligibility | Candidate, needs full text and human decision | Validation-only/contextual candidate; human decision pending |
**Novelty implication:** field validation is not new per se (Bonet reports a field trial); large-scale prediction accuracy is not proof of farmer adoption (Bernhardt). The proposed review can make a defensible contribution only if it systematically distinguishes recommendation availability, operational implementation, measured outcomes and sustained uptake across a comprehensive corpus. No claim that this is the first such review is justified yet.
**Methodological risk:** selection of only positive irrigation savings overstates evidence. Preserve null yield results, missing uptake measures, uncertainty and counterfactual comparators. Avoid conflating aggregate model-vs-measurement accuracy with implemented DSS impact.
Sources: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agwat.2026.110131 ; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agwat.2026.110730.
