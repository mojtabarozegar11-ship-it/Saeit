# MR-2026-001 — Novelty stress test: separating model validity, intent and adoption
Prepared 2026-10-24. This is an **argument audit**, not proof of publication-level novelty.

## Evidence collision risk
Prior reviews already cover irrigation scheduling, precision agriculture and farmer adoption; a narrative summary that merely juxtaposes these topics is likely redundant. The targeted 2024–2026 seed papers make the construct collision concrete:
1. Nxumalo et al. (DOI 10.3389/fagro.2025.1697188) show very high model agreement with a FAO-56-derived target and a proposed advisory prototype, not measured post-advice farmer water savings.
2. Monteiro et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-04775-x) measure **intentions** toward WST, not observed DSS use.
3. Wang & Xu (DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-76620-6) measure survey-reported WSIT adoption, without confirmed DSS-specific field water measurements.
4. Amini et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-26567-z) offer a farmer-participatory on-farm DSS pilot with field measurements, but cannot alone demonstrate sustained voluntary adoption.

## Candidate differentiator to test, not assert
Map each study by a two-dimensional **deployment/adoption ladder × outcome provenance** and audit whether the *same* study documents a traceable chain: advice delivered -> actual decision -> independent repeat use -> metered field water outcome. This might add value beyond technology-centered reviews **only if** a reproducible corpus-level comparison establishes that prior reviews did not already answer the question.

## Falsification criteria
Novelty is not established if an existing review already provides the same study-level actor/provenance crosswalk; if the database search yields too few genuinely relevant farmer-facing DSS deployments to sustain a useful evidence map; or if independent specialist assessment identifies a narrower already-published synthesis. If falsified, revise the research question or stop submission rather than invent novelty.
