## Direct novelty challenge and exact source claims
The study evaluated a Local Irrigation Advisory Service (LIAS) in the Genil–Cabra irrigation scheme in southern Spain during the **first five years of advice**. Its abstract explicitly compares **actual irrigation applied** against **LIAS recommendations** using **ARIS_LIAS (Annual Relative Irrigation Supply)** and describes substantial between-farmer heterogeneity. The abstract reports approximate ARIS_LIAS values of **0.23 for wheat and sunflower** and **0.94 for maize**. On the stated ratio definition these correspond to applied/recommended annual irrigation ratios of approximately 23% and 94%, **not** percentages of farmers who followed advice, not measured causal water savings, and not event-by-event adherence rates. The paper also reports economic indicators IWP and IWB; the abstract lists approximate IWP 0.23 €/m³ for wheat/sunflower/maize, 0.53 €/m³ for cotton/sugar beet, and >2 €/m³ for garlic under optimal schedules, and IWB 0.06 and 0.13 €/m³ for wheat and sunflower. These are abstract-reported values, not independently recomputed findings.

**Adversarial conclusion:** The broad proposition that no prior study compared recommendations with farmers' actual irrigation is **false**. Lorite et al. (2012) did so explicitly, more than a decade before this draft. Darouich et al. (2024) provides a second direct comparator. MR must not claim first identification of the advice–action gap or first use of actual/recommended irrigation ratios.

## Inference boundary and residual, UNVALIDATED contribution
- ARIS_LIAS is an annual/crop-scale relative supply measure; its abstract does not show timestamped recommendation delivery, field-metered execution for each event, a credible counterfactual causal effect, or post-project voluntary use.
- Nor does lack of such details in an **abstract** establish that the full paper omitted them. No negative claim about uninspected methods is permissible.
- MR's possible incremental contribution is only a **source-level claim-provenance and recommendation-event reporting template** separating advice, delivery, implementation, physical outcome, counterfactual and persistence, benchmarked against Lorite (2012), Ara (2021), Baroni (2019), Darouich (2024) and existing reviews. This template is **not yet independently validated** for inter-rater reliability or incremental utility.
