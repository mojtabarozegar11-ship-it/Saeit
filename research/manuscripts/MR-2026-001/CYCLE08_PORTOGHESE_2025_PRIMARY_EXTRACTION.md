# MR-2026-001 | Cycle 08 | Primary-source full-text extraction: Portoghese et al. (2025)
Source: Portoghese I, Matarrese R, Mirra L, Giannoccaro G. Assimilating Farmers’ Behaviour in the Development of an ET-Based Irrigation Water-Accounting Model. Water Resources Management 39:7749–7774 (2025). DOI 10.1007/s11269-025-04316-1. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11269-025-04316-1
Status: targeted calibration from publisher full text, NOT formally screened/included under frozen systematic protocol.
Design / provenance: Apulia (Italy) farmer survey, administered Dec 2018–Feb 2019 about 2017 irrigation season (§3.2). 300 farmers approached; 263 completed questionnaires; 79 single-crop farms used for IRMAT simulation (§3.4); authors disclose 19 metered farms in validation subset (§5). Survey responses = self-report (S); model estimates = P; 19 metered farms = metering-based reference (M) for validation, not observed DSS uptake (O).
Method: IRMAT soil-water-balance model assimilates farmer irrigation-behavior coefficients and is compared against self-reported volumes and FAO CROPWAT crop-water requirements (§3.1–3.4). Reported irrigation water abstraction is not equivalent to theoretical crop water requirement (§2). Conveyance/distribution efficiency neglected in IRMAT (§3.3).
Selected publisher-reported outcomes:
- 191 of 263 (rounded by authors to 73%) reported knowledge of their on-farm irrigation volume (§4.1, Fig. 3).
- IRMAT estimates correlated with the metered subset at R² = 0.76 (§4.2, Fig. 4), but this is a model-validation association, NOT percent water saved.
- Processing-tomato IRMAT estimates reportedly underestimated metered abstraction by 54% even against an upper prediction boundary (§4.2, Fig. 5); reference denominator must be confirmed before treating as quantitative effect.
- Economic illustration in §5: EUR 889 water-cost savings vs EUR 790.5 turnover loss (from another study); these are cited contextual estimates, not measured net gains attributable to IRMAT.
Critical limitations: recall lag from 2017 to interviews in 2018–19; selected single-crop subset; 19 metered references; unobserved longitudinal adherence; potential selection and self-report error; no causal effect of a farmer-facing irrigation advisory system measured. Paper itself cautions that farmers may already be deficit-irrigating.
Eligibility verdict: **BORDERLINE/CONTEXTUAL ONLY** under farmer-facing advisory DSS scope; water-accounting model is not demonstrated as a recommendation service delivered to and followed by growers. Do not count as included DSS adoption study without explicit human screening decision and tool-exposure verification.
