# MR-2026-001 — Source audit 07: Monteiro, Bahta & Jordaan (2025)
Status: targeted full-text calibration, not systematic inclusion. Checked 2026-10-24.
Primary version of record: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-04775-x
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-04775-x. Scientific Reports 15, article 23277 (2 July 2025). PubMed PMID 40603933: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40603933/

## Study and observed data
- Cross-sectional face-to-face questionnaires from **131 maize irrigators** in the Orange-Riet Irrigation Scheme, South Africa, drawn from a reported population of 371 commercial maize irrigators. See *Methods and data > Sampling technique and data collection*.
- Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB); partial least-squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM); Likert-type attitudinal measures.
- Measured construct: **self-reported intention to adopt water-saving technologies**, not actual uptake of a specified irrigation DSS, recommendation adherence, water-metered savings or post-adoption yield.
- The publisher reports positive association of attitude with intention (beta 0.306, p<0.01) and subjective norms with intention (beta 0.245, p<0.05); perceived behavioural control not significant in their model. These are the original authors' reported associations, not independently re-estimated effects.
- All 131 respondents were male according to the published demographic summary; limits generalization. Cross-sectional self-report cannot establish subsequent adoption or causal effect.
- The study's umbrella intervention is water-saving technologies (WST), not necessarily computerized irrigation advice; no DSS recommendation-use event was extracted from the examined sections.

## Methodological boundary
Classification: S-intention (survey self-report) with NO demonstrated DSS exposure, O-adherence, field water volume M, or sustained use. Candidate **contextual behavior literature**; not an included DSS study without verified DSS-specific subgroup.
The published paper itself explicitly states that intentions are not actual adoption and calls for longitudinal follow-up (Conclusions/limitations). Avoid turning its intent association into a claim that DSS saves water.
Ethics: study reports UFS-HSD2023/1327 approval and consent; do not transfer this approval to MR-2026-001.

## QA and follow-up
Check sampling-frame coverage, response/nonresponse, measurement validity, and whether any supplementary item isolates digital irrigation DSS. Independently reproduce only the displayed sample-size arithmetic (see CYCLE06_NUMERIC_QA.py). No raw survey data were obtained, and no human second reviewer has signed off.
