# MR-2026-001 C66 — Genil–Cabra evidence-cluster dependence audit

**2026-10-28 | Internal critical review | MAJOR REVISION / NO SUBMISSION**

## New cross-publication comparison (bibliographic and accessible abstract evidence)
The following reports concern the **same named irrigation district, Genil–Cabra, Spain**, and have overlapping author teams. They must **not** automatically be counted as independent site replications or pooled as independent samples.

| Source | Accessible description | Overlap signal | What is NOT established |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lorite, García-Vila, Carmona, Santos & Soriano (2012), DOI 10.1007/s11269-012-0023-3 | Five years of local advisory-service assessment; actual/recommended irrigation ratio (ARIS_LIAS), crop-level examples ≈0.23 wheat/sunflower and ≈0.94 maize | Genil–Cabra scheme, shared Lorite/Santos/García-Vila/Carmona team | Whether same plots, meters, years or individual growers appear in 2013 telemetry study |
| Lorite, Santos, García-Vila, Carmona & Fereres (2013), DOI 10.1016/j.compag.2013.08.007 | Two seasons of wireless telemetry across 1,365 plots and ≈7,500 ha | Same district and four named authors | Which exact years, plot IDs, overlap with 2012 advisory records, and whether each plot was an independent treatment unit |
| García-Vila, Lorite, Soriano & Fereres (2008), DOI 10.1016/j.agwat.2007.09.002 **[DOI must be verified against publisher before citation]** | Multi-year scheme-level irrigation management and ARIS trends; ≈7,000 ha | Same district and author team | Whether its source records are reused in 2012 or 2013 analyses |
| Santos, Lorite, Tasumi, Allen & Fereres (2010), bibliographic record at FAO AGRIS | Remote-sensing and water-balance assessment at Genil–Cabra, 2004–2005 | Same district and overlapping researchers | Whether field identifiers/data overlap the other three papers |

**Evidence boundary:** Co-location and shared authorship are confirmed from accessible records. **Actual sample duplication is a hypothesis, not a finding.** Different collection years, measurement protocols, crops and endpoints may make the studies complementary rather than redundant. Until full methods and dates are obtained, treat them as **one geographic evidence cluster with unresolved statistical dependence**, not four independent replications of DSS efficacy.

## Consequences for MR
1. Do not add plot totals or farmer counts across these studies. The 1,365 plots of 2013 are not 1,365 independent adoption events or 1,365 experimental treatment units.
2. Do not interpret 2012 ARIS_LIAS as event-level farmer compliance or 2013 telemetry as evidence of message receipt or recommendation uptake.
3. Do not meta-analyze effect sizes across the four reports without full-text study-period, site, plot-ID, treatment-unit and outcome-provenance checks.
4. The literature already contains actual-versus-recommended irrigation indicators and telemetry; any novel MR claim must rest on a genuinely distinguishable and independently evaluated G/D/R/A traceability approach.
