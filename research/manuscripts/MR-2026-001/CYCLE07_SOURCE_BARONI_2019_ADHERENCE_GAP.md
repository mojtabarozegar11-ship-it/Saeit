# MR-2026-001 | Cycle 07 | Baroni et al. (2019) source extraction
**Source level:** Wiley publisher abstract; no access to full article methods, raw interview transcript or tables. Targeted seed, not PRISMA-included.
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ird.2337
DOI (publisher verified): 10.1002/ird.2337. Gabriele Baroni, Katrin Drastig, Anna-Ulrike Lichtenfeld, Leonie Jost, Peter Claas. *Irrigation and Drainage* 68(3):520–530. Published online 4 April 2019.
Title: Assessment of Irrigation Scheduling Systems in Germany: Survey of the Users and Comparative Study.
Source location: Abstract, lines describing methods and findings.
**Methods:** Six irrigation scheduling methods were identified; researchers conducted direct end-user interviews and comparative assessment.
**Reported findings:** users expressed favorable feedback, yet recommendations were rarely implemented. The seasonal irrigation requirement was used to support actual water abstraction, while within-season schedules and number of irrigation events differed more among systems.
**Construct provenance:** interview responses = S (reported opinion/use); recommendation adherence is a source-level qualitative finding from interviews, NOT time-stamped independent field logs. Cross-system calculation agreement = comparative modeling/estimation, not a metered farm-level water-saving effect. No quantified adherence rate or sample size was accessible in abstract.
**Novelty implication:** a direct historical example of an attitude–action gap in irrigation scheduling, making it inappropriate to infer adoption from positive attitudes. Review must test whether prior syntheses already mapped this gap before claiming novelty.
**Next:** obtain full text; extract sample and sampling method, exact adherence questions, direct observation or logs, seasonal comparator and uncertainty. No numerical effect or DOI beyond publisher evidence invented.
