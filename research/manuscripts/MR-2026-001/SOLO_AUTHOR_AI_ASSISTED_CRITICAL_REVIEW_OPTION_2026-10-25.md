## Why this option is necessary
The JBI Manual, current guidance for forming a scoping-review team (updated 2026-08-31), states that the scoping review is a team activity and cannot be conducted by only one person; at least two real people should select and extract evidence. Its selection guidance emphasizes independent dual screening, and treats single-reviewer screening as generally not recommended outside a rapid-scoping context. Link: https://jbi-global-wiki.refined.site/space/MANUAL/2042298373/10.2.1.1%2BForming%2Bthe%2Bright%2Bteam and https://jbi-global.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/MANUAL/pages/355862769/10.2.7%20Data%20extraction .

**AI is not a second human researcher or author.** Elsevier (updated June 2026) and Springer Nature require human accountability, scientific contribution and AI-use transparency; never designate ChatGPT as a human reviewer, author or coauthor. Policies: https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals ; https://group.springernature.com/gp/group/ai/ai-guidance-for-researchers-editors-reviewers .

## Two ethically viable paths; keep author decision explicit
**Path A — retain Scoping Review:** engage at least one *genuine* qualified second person (e.g. agricultural water scientist, research librarian or evidence-synthesis methodologist) who actually independently screens evidence and participates scientifically as appropriate. Give proper acknowledgment/authorship only if genuine contributions satisfy the publisher's rules; no purchased nominal authors or fabricated signatures. Continue PRISMA-ScR and JBI-style study-selection. The current 25-source pilot is a ready-to-assign starting point but 0 genuine human screening entries currently exist.

**Path B — ONLY one human author, no third party (recommended feasible option):** develop a **Critical Narrative Review / Evidence-Informed Perspective**. Not a Scoping Review, not a Systematic Review, and not an attempt to describe screening decisions as independent. Prospective working title:
*From Irrigation Recommendations to Field Outcomes: A Critical Review of Validation Evidence and Farmer Uptake under Water Scarcity*.

This narrower synthesis maps methodological differences in evidence for farmer-facing actionable irrigation advice across real farms, distinguishing actual measured water use, self-reported decisions and satisfaction, experimental adherence and scenario/model estimates. The author does a genuine original critical interpretation and checks original source articles. The AI assistant assists with discovery, reference organization, verification, reproducible computations, outlines and language drafting; the author reviews, substantively revises, decides and signs the final manuscript. The final manuscript must disclose AI assistance per selected publisher policy.

### Submission caveats
- **Not guaranteed publishable or acceptable as Review Article in any named journal.** Verify the specific intended journal currently accepts **single-author narrative/critical reviews**; some accept review manuscripts only by invitation or require systematic methodology. Do not infer a journal's acceptance of this article type from general publisher websites.
- Full author substantive contribution is still necessary; AI-only article generation or a superficial signoff is not sufficient.
- No clinical-style PRISMA flow, meta-analysis, systematic completeness claims or invented human reviewer agreement. Do describe exact executed searching and limits honestly.
- A sole-author paper is possible in many academic contexts, but journal scope/editorial standards determine outcome and no acceptance can be promised.
- Ethical verification still requires all publisher declarations, copyright and overlap checks, accurate institutional affiliation, permitted reuse, final author version/fee approval, and actual publisher receipt.
