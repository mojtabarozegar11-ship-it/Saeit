# MR-2026-001 | Cycle 08 | Evidence quality: direct advice vs inferred use
Sources: Portoghese et al. (2025) https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11269-025-04316-1 ; Tapsuwan S, Hunink J, Alcon F, Mertens-Palomares AN, Baille A. (2015, online Nov 2014), Irrigation and Drainage 64(2):228–240, DOI 10.1002/ird.1887, publisher abstract https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ird.1887 .
Portoghese: data collection is farmer self-report (S), IRMAT and CROPWAT are model outputs (P), small subset of water meters provides reference for model validation (M). No direct observation of farmer adherence to a delivered DSS recommendation (O) was verified. R²=0.76 is model agreement, not irrigation savings. Contextual only pending scope approval.
Tapsuwan: irrigation advisory bulletin in Spain's Segura River Basin distributed to innovative early-adopter farmers; formative evaluation used pre/post-trial surveys to identify acceptability obstacles (relevance, flexibility, access, confidence, institutional/political barriers) and discrepancies in information needs. This is an actual farmer-facing advisory intervention, making it a **candidate for formal screening**. Publisher abstract does NOT report respondent count, water metering, measured savings, controlled comparator, independent adoption denominator or sustained uptake; all remain UNKNOWN, not zero.
Contrast: farmer feedback about acceptability (S) != observed advice adherence (O); technical model performance (P) != field water saving (M); water-meter validation (M) != a causal effect of advisory deployment.
Bias appraisal for future full text: selection of early adopters; self-report and social desirability; no known counterfactual; unknown loss to follow-up; missing field meter details; recall lag in Portoghese; potential clustering by farm/season. No validated risk-of-bias score assigned without full design and protocol.
Scientific consequence: chart each outcome at its actual observation level and retain explicit UNVERIFIED flags. Neither study is currently counted as included in a systematic corpus. Tapsuwan full-text access is still required.
