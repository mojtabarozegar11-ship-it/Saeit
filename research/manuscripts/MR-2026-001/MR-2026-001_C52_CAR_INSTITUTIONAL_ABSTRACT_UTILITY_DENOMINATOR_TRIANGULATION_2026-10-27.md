## Fresh, distinct arithmetic and estimand test
- **45/72 = 62.5000%**: all-season return of irrigation/rainfall information; published abstract rounds to 63%. It is **not** event-specific following of advice.
- **(45+13)/72 = 58/72 = 80.5556%**: any reported return of data among the three abstract categories. This is **not** a source-confirmed definition of the paper's reported overall 80% perceived usefulness.
- **(45+6)/72 = 51/72 = 70.8333%**: if one were to add only six retrospectively regretful half-season discontinuers to all-season reporters, this would **not** reproduce 80%. The abstract says six of the thirteen who stopped later regretted stopping; it does not state that regret is the exact numerical construction of the overall 80% utility claim.
- The source's *overall 80% usefulness* is a separate **author-inferred utility construct**, not the 58/72 observed any-return rate unless source methods explicitly establish that equivalence.
- The earlier **C51** six-category Table 5 transcription totals **74**, not 72; its apparent inconsistency is **not** resolved by this independently accessible abstract. The Table 6 narrative/table 40-versus-41 issue also remains open.

## Author-facing corrective decision
Do not say the abstract's 80% usefulness figure is *calculated from* the six regretful respondents, the 58 users returning some data, or a Table 5 category until original publisher methods establish the denominator and decision rule. Report **45/72** as sustained *data return*, **58/72** only as a separately calculated any-return descriptor, and **80%** solely as the paper's reported subjective utility inference, with uncertain denominator provenance. The paper's SMS logs and user feedback cannot independently establish irrigation-event adherence or metered water saving.
