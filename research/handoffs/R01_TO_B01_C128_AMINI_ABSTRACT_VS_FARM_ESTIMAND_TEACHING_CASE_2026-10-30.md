# R-01 → B-01 | C128 textbook insert: same number, different estimand
Date 2026-10-30. Deliverable: ready-to-review scientific teaching case for irrigation/agricultural economics books. **Not yet integrated or published by B-01.**

### Case text
Amini et al. (2025) report a 3.87% drip-irrigation water-productivity improvement in their abstract, but the F5 orchard section is described in the Results as an approximately 3.8 kg/m³ *absolute* increase. Their Table 4 displays 8.4 versus 4.6 kg/m³ for treatment versus control; subtracting these rounded values yields +3.8 kg/m³, whereas dividing that difference by the control yields +82.61% (rounded-value relative contrast). Table 5 prints +3.87 under a percentage-labeled column. These figures must not be silently combined as if they have the same unit, aggregation level or denominator. The study's source data would be needed to reconstruct an exact system-level weighted percentage.

**Student exercise:** Identify the unit (kg/m³ versus %), the unit of analysis (farm section versus irrigation-system aggregate), the comparator, the difference between absolute and relative change, and why identical digits are not proof of identical estimands. **Answer:** +3.8 kg/m³ is the displayed F5 absolute contrast; +82.61% is a derived rounded-value relative contrast; 3.87% is an author-reported drip-system abstract claim whose weights are not verified here. None of these is an independently estimated causal effect or an event-level compliance rate.

**Source:** Amini A, Emami S, Dehghanisanij H (2025), *Scientific Reports* 15:42480. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-26567-z (Abstract, Tables 4–5 and Results). Source uncertainty must remain visible in any textbook treatment.
