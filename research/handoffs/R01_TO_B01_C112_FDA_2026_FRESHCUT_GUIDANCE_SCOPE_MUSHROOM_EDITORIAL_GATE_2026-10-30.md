## Source and date
FDA issued **final guidance on 11 August 2026**, *Guide to Minimize Biological Hazards in Ready-to-Eat Fresh-Cut Produce* (Docket FDA-2018-D-3583), replacing the 2008 fresh-cut guidance. This is a **guidance document** about implementation of existing 21 CFR part 117 requirements, not a new universal mushroom-processing law or an independent safety certification.

## Insert-ready editorial correction
“FDA's August 2026 final guidance addresses **ready-to-eat, high-water-activity (>0.85), fresh-cut produce** processed and distributed in fresh form, and explains potential biological-hazard controls under 21 CFR part 117. The guidance defines fresh-cut produce by physical alteration of a fresh fruit or vegetable from its whole state (such as slicing or dicing), **without additional processing such as freezing, cooking or canning**. Its stated intended audience is **manufacturers/processors**, including covered farm mixed-type facilities; the guidance says it is **not intended for farms as such**. Its recommendations do not independently establish legally enforceable duties unless a cited statute or regulation does so. A mushroom enterprise therefore needs a documented **product form, intended consumption, processing operation, water activity, establishment classification and jurisdiction** before a specialist determines whether particular guidance examples are pertinent. This document alone does not justify saying that all whole, dried, frozen, cooked or cultivated mushrooms are covered, nor that an environmental detection automatically mandates recall of every lot.”
