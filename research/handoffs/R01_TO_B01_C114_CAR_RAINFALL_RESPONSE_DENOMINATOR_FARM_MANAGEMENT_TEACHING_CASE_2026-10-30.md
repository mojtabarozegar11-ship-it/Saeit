## Insert-ready case: denominators change the question

Car et al. (2012) evaluated an Australian SMS irrigation advisory system. Their original Table 4 reports two distinct rainfall events:

| Event (2008) | Growers using system during event | Growers sending rainfall-input SMS | Response fraction | What it actually measures |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 7–8 November | 54 | 51 | 51/54 = **94.44%** | Reporting of rainfall input by event-specific active users |
| 12–14 December | 72 | 54 | 54/72 = **75.00%** | Reporting of rainfall input by a larger active-user frame |

A manager must not call these values a 94% or 75% *irrigation-advice compliance rate*: the numerator is a **rainfall message**, not verified execution of a recommended irrigation event. Nor do two different denominators prove that response by the same growers changed over time. The original authors also note that rainfall varied spatially and some growers may not have experienced a reportable rain event. Therefore missing rainfall SMS cannot be equated with a failure to adopt advice. The original Table 5 additionally lists water-balance groups whose printed counts sum to **74** despite a printed total of **72**; the present case does not repair that contradiction.

**Worked assessment exercise (with answer):** A consultant claims 'rainfall-event reporting dropped 19.44 percentage points, so adoption declined by that amount.' The arithmetic difference (94.44−75.00=19.44 percentage points) is correct **only for two event-specific reporting fractions**; the inference about within-grower adoption is invalid without linked participant IDs, rainfall exposure, equal eligibility and observed advice/action data. A sound evaluation separately tracks (a) who was eligible at each event, (b) whether rain occurred at that holding, (c) whether a report was sent, (d) the actual recommendation, (e) independent irrigation application and (f) subsequent farm outcomes.
