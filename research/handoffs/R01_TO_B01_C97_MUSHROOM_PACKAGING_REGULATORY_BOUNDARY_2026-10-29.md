## Insertable English teaching passage (original editorial text)

Mushroom packaging is a **hazard-control problem**, not merely a shelf-life, sensory-quality or waste-reduction problem. In the United States, **21 CFR 112.115** expressly requires that covered produce be packaged so as to prevent formation of *Clostridium botulinum* toxin when it is a known or reasonably foreseeable hazard, with mushrooms named as an example. This rule should not be represented as a universal substitute for processed-food controls. A shelf-stable, hermetically sealed mushroom product may also fall within the separate **low-acid canned food (21 CFR Part 113)** or **acidified-food (21 CFR Part 114)** frameworks, depending on the actual finished equilibrium pH, water activity, processing and product classification. FDA guidance explains that commercial processors of covered shelf-stable low-acid/acidified products sold in the US may need establishment registration and a filed scheduled process under **21 CFR Part 108** for each relevant product, style, container and process. These requirements cannot be replaced by a home recipe, a nominal label claim, a shelf-life simulation, a visual seal inspection or an unvalidated pH assumption. A competent processing authority and the applicable regulator must establish product-specific processing requirements before commercial use. This educational chapter **does not supply a thermal schedule, validated pH control, sterile assurance, licence or release approval**.
