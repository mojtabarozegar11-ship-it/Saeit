## Original source and safe reading
Amini, Emami and Dehghanisanij (2025), *Scientific Reports* 15, article 42480, DOI [10.1038/s41598-025-26567-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-26567-z). In Methods, “Collecting data on water flow,” irrigation inflow is measured by WSC flumes for surface irrigation and nozzle/dripper discharge for pressurized irrigation. Equation (3) defines **WP = Y/I**, with yield Y (kg/ha) and *applied irrigation* I (m³/ha). This is irrigation-input productivity, **not** directly measured evapotranspiration, net groundwater depletion, or water retained in a river basin.

The publisher's F7 apple orchard treatment used **292.7 mm more** applied irrigation than its customary control. The article gives conflicting exact F7 water-productivity decreases: 1.3 kg/m³ in one Results passage, 1.4 kg/m³ in the later Results discussion, and 0.1 kg/m³ in the Conclusion. Its displayed Table 5 values (4.6 and 6.0 kg/m³) differ by −1.4 kg/m³, but rounded values cannot identify the exact unrounded contrast. The abstract's surface-system **1.5% average** is a reported aggregate, not proof that F7 improved.

## Classroom problem — worked, transparent and unit-correct
One millimetre applied to one hectare is 0.001 m × 10,000 m² = **10 m³/ha**. Thus the F7 contrast **292.7 mm × 10 = 2,927 m³/ha** of *additional applied irrigation* in the DSS section. At a **hypothetical** marginal delivered-water charge of `p` currency units/m³, the irrigation-charge increment would be **2,927p currency units/ha**, *conditional on volumetric billing and equal other conditions*. For illustration only, at hypothetical `p = 0.05`, the charge difference would be **146.35 currency units/ha**. This is **not** a measured financial cost, profit difference or social value of depleted water in the original study.

**Discussion:** A farm can have greater crop yield and still lower irrigation-input productivity if the irrigation denominator rises faster than the yield numerator. Distinguish delivered irrigation, actual ET, return flow, deep drainage, farm revenue, water opportunity cost and basin conservation. The original article does not provide sufficient F7 financial and hydrologic observations to calculate the latter quantities.
