## Evidence-backed teaching insert

A farmer may install pressure irrigation equipment without adopting a scientific irrigation-scheduling practice. Conversely, a farmer may consult scientific scheduling guidance without installing a new irrigation system or following every recommendation. Fan and McCann (2020) analyze the US 2013 Farm and Ranch Irrigation Survey using multilevel models and distinguish adoption of pressure systems from adoption of scientific scheduling practices; reported associations differ at state and farm levels. Such associations are **not causal intervention effects** or meter-verified water savings. A teaching exercise should therefore record separately (a) equipment ownership, (b) scheduling-practice use, (c) actual action on a dated recommendation, and (d) metered water/energy outcomes, with each denominator and observation date.

Car et al. (2012) provide an additional denominator-integrity exercise: 72 enrolled; 45/13/14 duration groups reconcile; original Table 5 six category counts sum to 74 despite a printed total of 72; Table 6 counts sum to 41 while narrative states 40 open-ended respondents. No correction to the source's participant roster can be inferred from those printed counts. The Table 6 46/73/80% progression concerns **reported behavior-influence categories**, not objective compliance. Do not infer cost-benefit, adoption persistence, water savings or equipment ROI from these fractions.
