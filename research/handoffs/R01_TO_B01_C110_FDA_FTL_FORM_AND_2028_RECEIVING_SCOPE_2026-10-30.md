## Source-verified corrections for the chapter
1. FDA's Food Traceability Rule applies only to specifically designated Food Traceability List (FTL) foods, listed-form ingredients, and covered entities/events subject to exemptions. Do **not** claim that every fresh, sliced, frozen or dried mushroom product is automatically FTL-covered; verify the actual commodity/form and exemptions with the current official list and competent regulatory reviewer. Food safety/receiving controls can remain prudent even when FTL-specific records are inapplicable.
2. FDA FAQ TFTL.12 (last updated **2024-03-20**) says that if a facility receives an FTL **fresh-cut fruit** and then freezes it, the facility keeps **receiving KDEs for the incoming fresh-cut product**, but after conversion to frozen form, no additional FTL records are required for that transformed non-FTL food; downstream handling of that frozen form is likewise outside that FTL category. Do not generalize this example to all foods (e.g., listed seafood is different) or automatically to mushrooms.
3. FDA states Congress directed non-enforcement of the FTL rule **before 2028-07-20**. This is not a repeal of the final rule, not a blanket exemption from existing food-safety law, and not proof that any operator is compliant. The small-retailer enforcement-discretion guidance discussed in October 2026 is **draft**, not a blanket effective exemption.

## Ready-to-insert replacement (subject to B specialist review)
“Traceability duties must be determined for the exact food category, physical form, activity and entity against the current FDA Food Traceability List and applicable exemptions. Where an incoming product is an FTL food, receiving-event records may be required even if subsequent processing changes the product into a form no longer listed; for example, FDA's FAQ explains this for fresh-cut fruit received before freezing. A receiving specification should separately document lot identity, source, date, temperature, contamination controls and product form for food-safety and recall readiness, without misrepresenting each record as an FTL mandate. The statutory non-enforcement period before 20 July 2028 must not be described as repeal or as waiver of other food-safety obligations.”
