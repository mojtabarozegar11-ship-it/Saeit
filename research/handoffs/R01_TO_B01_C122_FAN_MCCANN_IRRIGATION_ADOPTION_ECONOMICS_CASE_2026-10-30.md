# R-01 → B-01 | Farm management teaching insert: technology adoption is not scheduling adoption
**Owner:** B-01 books. **Status:** READY_FOR_REVIEW, original teaching text, not inserted or published. **Date:** 2026-10-30.

## Reader-ready passage
A farm can install pressure-irrigation equipment without adopting scientific irrigation scheduling. The first decision concerns a physical irrigation system; the second concerns a recurring information and management practice. They have different adoption costs, learning requirements and observational indicators. In the US 2013 Farm and Ranch Irrigation Survey, Fan and McCann (2020) used multilevel models and found that variation in pressure-system adoption was associated more strongly with state-level factors, whereas scientific scheduling adoption was associated more strongly with farm-level factors. These are statistical associations, not randomized effects or proof of actual water conservation.

A useful farm-management record therefore separates (1) hardware installed, (2) scheduling tools actually used, (3) recommendations delivered, (4) irrigation actions documented, and (5) measured outcomes. Adoption of one item must not be substituted for evidence of another.

## Teaching exercise
A cooperative records that 60 members own drip equipment and that 25 members report using soil-moisture-based scheduling. Can the cooperative infer 25 of the 60 drip owners use scientific scheduling? **No:** the overlap is not known. If the total cooperative has 100 members, the overlap could range from max(0,60+25−100)=0 to min(60,25)=25. Can it infer water savings? **No:** no before/after volumes, weather, comparator or causal design are supplied. The numbers are a hypothetical arithmetic exercise, not data from Fan and McCann.
