# R-01 → B-01 — mushroom traceability source correction (C103)
**Date:** 2026-10-29. **Status:** evidence-based editorial handoff, READY_FOR_REVIEW by B-01; not a legal opinion, regulatory clearance or live-site change. Target: Mushroom EN Vol II, Ch22 source classification and Ch30 traceability language.

## Two distinct regulatory questions must not be conflated
**Product coverage** depends on the exact food form and the FDA Food Traceability List (FTL), not the generic word “mushroom.” The [FDA FTL rule overview](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods) covers specified listed foods and foods containing listed ingredients remaining in the listed form. The [FDA FTL/FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/frequently-asked-questions-fsma-food-traceability-rule) describes **fresh-cut non-leafy vegetables** as a category and excludes foods that cease to be fresh through freezing/drying; FDA's produce definition includes mushrooms ([21 CFR 112.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/112.1)). This supports a **potentially covered fresh-cut mushroom** classification but is not an item-specific written FDA determination; product/process, exemption and distribution facts must be checked.

**Enforcement timing** is a separate question. FDA's [June 15, 2026 public meeting background](https://www.fda.gov/food/workshops-meetings-webinars-food-and-dietary-supplements/fda-public-meeting-challenges-and-solutions-lot-level-food-traceability-06152026) states that the 2026 Continuing Appropriations Act directed the FDA **not to enforce the FTR before 20 July 2028**, and that FDA intends to comply. This is stronger than describing July 2028 as *merely a proposed extension*, but it does **not** remove the underlying traceability framework or other food-safety duties. Do not quote the outdated 20 January 2026 date as a current enforcement deadline.

## Editorial decision matrix — keep all three forms separate
| Product form | Editorial classification for US FSMA 204 FTL | Exact source/evidence gate | Safe book wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole intact fresh edible mushrooms | Not individually named in the common whole-fresh FTL produce categories; do **not** infer universal coverage or exemption | Confirm actual product and current official FTL/exemptions | “Coverage must be evaluated by listed food form and applicable exemptions.” |
| Fresh sliced/cut mushrooms | **Potential FTL fresh-cut non-leafy vegetable**, because FDA produce definition includes mushrooms; final applicability not legally certified | Confirm fresh-cut definition, processing steps, 21 CFR 1.1305 exemptions and FDA clarification if needed | “Fresh-cut mushrooms may fall within the fresh-cut non-leafy vegetable category; verify product-specific applicability.” |
| Frozen/dried/cooked mushroom product | A fresh-only FTL category generally ceases to apply once form is changed; a mixed/other listed ingredient can alter analysis | Check exact ingredients, treatment, and other independently applicable food safety rules | “Changing a fresh-only food's form can change FTL coverage; it does not eliminate all food-safety obligations.” |

## Proposed corrections to B manuscript (do not edit B-owned source)
1. Replace any blanket “all mushrooms are FTL foods” or “all mushrooms exempt” claim with the above form-dependent decision rule.
2. Replace any statement that the Food Traceability Rule **is currently enforced in 2026** with the dated FDA 2026 non-enforcement directive, while retaining voluntary readiness and other safety obligations.
3. Distinguish legal coverage, voluntary best-practice lot traceability and actual safety validation. A sample traceability log is not a legal certification, and 24-hour retrieval capability must be tested with real authorized records before claimed.
4. For any cited study or regulatory rule, preserve its source URL, access date, version and a dated reviewer decision. No fabricated product approval.
