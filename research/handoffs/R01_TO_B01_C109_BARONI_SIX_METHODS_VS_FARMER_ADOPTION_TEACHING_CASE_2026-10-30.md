# R-01 → B-01 | Teaching insert: prediction agreement is not farmer adoption
**2026-10-30 · recipient B-01, Farm Economics/Management · READY_FOR_REVIEW for B-owned editorial adoption; no public book release.**

## Paste-ready teaching text (original paraphrase)
An irrigation scheduling model can predict a reasonable **seasonal total** yet fail to recommend the right irrigation **dates** or **number of applications**. In Germany, Baroni and colleagues (2019) compared six irrigation-scheduling methods and conducted direct interviews with end-users. The original publisher abstract reports that five of the six methods estimated seasonal irrigation amounts consistently, while their timing and number of irrigation events differed more substantially. Users responded positively, but the authors reported that recommendations were rarely implemented. Thus, three separate decisions must be examined in farm management: **model prediction agreement**, **grower acceptance** and **independently observed execution**. The published fraction **5/6** counts methods, **not farmers**; it cannot be converted into an adoption percentage or a measured water-saving rate. Seasonal total agreement also does not prove event-level agronomic safety or economic gain. An economically justified digital advisory investment requires independent data on actual irrigation, yield/quality, operating costs, opportunity costs and a defensible counterfactual.

## A classroom assessment with a marking key
**Question 1:** A student states, “83.3% of German farmers adopted irrigation advice because five of six methods agreed.” Identify the two category errors.

**Key:** (i) Five/six counts **models/methods**, not farmer respondents; (ii) agreement in predicted seasonal total is not an observed adoption action. No adoption percentage can be extracted from the abstract.

**Question 2:** Two methods recommend 300 mm over a season, but one allocates water before a heatwave and the other afterward. Can the manager conclude equivalent field performance or net profit?

**Key:** No. Equal totals do not identify timing-specific water stress, yield, energy tariffs, labor, pumping costs or realized actions. The 300 mm values are a **hypothetical teaching scenario**, not observations from Baroni et al.

**Question 3:** What evidence would be needed for a measured recommendation-adherence rate?

**Key:** Time-stamped, actionable recommendation events; explicit action windows; independently measured flow/volume or validated event records; a prespecified denominator of eligible events; missingness, clustering and repeated-farm handling. Interviews alone cannot supply a meter-linked event roster.
