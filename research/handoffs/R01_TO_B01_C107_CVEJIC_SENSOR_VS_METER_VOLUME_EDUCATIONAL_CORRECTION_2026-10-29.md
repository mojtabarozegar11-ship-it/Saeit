# R-01 → B-01 scientific handoff: sensor precision ≠ measured water conservation
**Owner of this independent handoff:** R-01 | **Receiving owner:** B-01 | **Date:** 2026-10-29 | **Stage:** FILE DELIVERED; B adoption PENDING

## Source-backed editorial correction
In [Cvejić et al. (2020), Methods and Results](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/10/9/1238), soil-moisture sensor RMSE improved from 3.8–14.2 to 0.4–2.9 **volumetric percentage points** after site-specific calibration (median 9.9→1.5). This verifies **soil-water-content sensing accuracy**, not irrigation discharge or actual volume savings. At baseline, the authors report only **26%** used water meters, **74%** had no water-consumption records and **91%** did not regularly maintain the initial irrigation diaries. The article's 25% irrigation-volume reduction is a **conditional simulation**, not independently metered savings from an observed treatment group.

## Replacement teaching paragraph for the B-01 irrigation/economics text
“Better calibration of a soil-moisture sensor improves the reliability of the soil-water-status information used for scheduling. It does not, by itself, measure how many cubic metres were applied or saved. Applied irrigation volume must be obtained from a calibrated flow meter or defensible discharge × runtime records, matched to area, dates and baseline conditions. When a study models 25% lower water use under a specified irrigation strategy, describe that as a simulated scenario, not an observed conservation result.”

## Fully labelled synthetic classroom calculation (NOT Cvejić field data)
At an illustrative fixed discharge of **10 L/min**, four irrigation events of **30 min** each use **1,200 L** in total. Six events of **20 min** each also use **1,200 L**. Shorter events can therefore coexist with *no* seasonal water-volume reduction when event frequency rises. A second hypothetical farm with six 15-minute events would use 900 L, but this would not establish causal savings without a comparable control and reliable metering.
