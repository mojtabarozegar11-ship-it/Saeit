## High-priority source-scope correction

Health Canada's *Guidance on Mitigating the Health Risk Associated with Clostridium botulinum in Plant and Mushroom Products Stored in Oil* expressly covers plant/mushroom matter **covered in oil, in sealed packages, offered at retail**. It is not a blanket rule for every fresh-mushroom tray or all mushroom packaging. Official scope: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/plant-mushroom-oil-profile/plant-mushroom-oil.html

For **commercial mushrooms in oil**, the guidance calls for validated controls (e.g. acidification, water activity, thermal process, combined hurdles), product-specific assessment, and appropriate labels. It explicitly cautions that prolonged refrigeration alone is not a universally sufficient control. Do not convert these parameters into an unvalidated home recipe, claim that a single generic pH/time formula covers all products, or imply a Canadian guidance document itself grants product certification.

For **home-prepared vegetables and mushrooms in oil**, Health Canada's separate consumer advice is to refrigerate and use within one week; this is consumer guidance, not a validated shelf-life for a commercial SKU: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-safety-fruits-vegetables/food-safety-tips-vegetables-herbs-oil.html

The Codex 2023 *General Principles of Food Hygiene* (CXC 1-1969), §14.1–14.4, addresses lot identification, traceability, product information, labeling and consumer education. It is a cross-cutting hygiene framework; it is **not** itself a product-specific mushroom-in-oil processing schedule. Primary publication: https://www.fao.org/3/cc6125en/cc6125en.pdf
