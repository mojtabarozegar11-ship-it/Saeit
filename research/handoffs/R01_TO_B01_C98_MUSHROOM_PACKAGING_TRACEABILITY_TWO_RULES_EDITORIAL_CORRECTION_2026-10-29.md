## Concrete correction for B-01 C92 Chapter 30.19–30.20
A mushroom packaging hazard rule and the additional FSMA 204 traceability recordkeeping rule must **not** be treated as interchangeable. They have different triggers and neither is satisfied merely by writing a traceability spreadsheet.

1. **Packaging hazard control (21 CFR 112.115).** For **covered produce**, packaging must prevent formation of *Clostridium botulinum* toxin when reasonably foreseeable, explicitly including mushrooms as an example. This is a product/process hazard-control requirement, **not** proof that every mushroom is on the FDA Food Traceability List and **not** a prescriptive safe oxygen percentage, shelf life or packaging recipe. Applicability and process validation must be assessed by a qualified specialist.
   - [21 CFR 112.115, current 2026 text](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-112/subpart-L/section-112.115)
   - [FDA fresh-mushroom FSVP enforcement example, 2024](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/green-day-produce-inc-679085-07112024) confirms that covered produce and importer verification are separate questions.
2. **Additional traceability records (21 CFR Part 1 Subpart S).** FDA says this rule covers specified **Food Traceability List (FTL)** foods and applicable critical tracking events/key data elements; it is not an automatic obligation for every food. FDA's official page states that Congress directed FDA **not to enforce this rule before 20 July 2028**; this does **not** suspend 21 CFR 112.115, importer FSVP obligations, other applicable food-safety requirements, or voluntary batch traceability good practice.
   - [FDA Food Traceability Final Rule and 2028 non-enforcement status, current October 2026](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/fsma-final-rule-requirements-additional-traceability-records-certain-foods)
   - [FDA FTL FAQ and form/processing distinctions](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-safety-modernization-act-fsma/frequently-asked-questions-fsma-food-traceability-rule)

## Proposed copy-ready editorial paragraph (original wording)
> A food's packaging hazard controls and its additional traceability obligations must be assessed separately. For covered fresh produce, including mushrooms where botulinum toxin is a reasonably foreseeable packaging hazard, 21 CFR 112.115 requires packaging that prevents toxin formation. Independently, FDA's Food Traceability Rule adds event-specific records only when the food and operation fall within its stated scope, subject to applicable exemptions. As of October 2026, FDA states that it will not enforce that additional rule before July 20, 2028; this timing does not eliminate other food-safety duties. No packaging atmosphere, shelf-life, microbial limit, or process-validation claim can be derived from this legal summary alone.
