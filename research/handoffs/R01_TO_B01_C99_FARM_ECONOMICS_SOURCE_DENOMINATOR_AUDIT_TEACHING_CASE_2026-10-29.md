# R-01 → B-01 | Farm Economics A: evidence-denominator audit teaching case (C99)

## Learning outcome
Students should be able to identify a non-conserving sample table, distinguish arithmetic consistency from observational validity, and refuse to infer irrigation-advice adherence or cost-benefit from engagement and self-report. This case is suitable as a **methods/evidence-quality exercise** in Farm Economics A, not as an agronomic treatment recommendation.

## Student exercise (original educational wording)
**Problem A — denominator conservation.** Table 5 prints six group counts: 26, 7, 3, 6, 15 and 17, with a printed total of 72. Calculate the sum and discrepancy. The UNE abstract separately reports 14 participants who never sent data. If both sources were describing the same participant roster and the 17 in the table were replaced by 14, would the six-row table then reconcile? Explain what cannot be inferred.

**Problem B — false percentage repair.** The source's prose states that Groups 1–3 represent 68%, but the printed counts of those groups are 26, 7 and 3. Using the printed 72-person total, calculate the actual fraction and explain why a hypothetical denominator selected to reproduce 68% cannot be treated as observed data.

**Problem C — response-frame sensitivity.** The prose reports 40 answers to an open-ended question while Table 6 contains five category counts of 11, 10, 9, 8 and 3. The authors combine two categories into 19 responses. Assuming **only for sensitivity illustration** that exactly one category assignment were duplicated, calculate the range of fractions for the combined 19 when the unknown duplicate is removed. State why this is not a confidence interval.

**Problem D — economic interpretation.** Why can neither daily SMS delivery nor an expressed intention to participate next season identify willingness to pay, measured water savings, or realized commercial retention? Name the minimum extra observations required for a defensible economic evaluation.

## Instructor key (do not silently replace original published values)
- **A:** Sum = 74, discrepancy +2 versus 72. Changing 17 to 14 produces 71, still one below 72. Under the shared-roster assumption, at least one other category must change or the roster assumption must fail; no particular correction is identified.
- **B:** 36/72 = 50.00%, not 68%. A denominator of 53 yields 36/53 ≈ 67.92%, but the article does not authenticate 53 as the Table 5 Groups 1–3 denominator. It would be data fabrication to present this as the corrected proportion.
- **C:** Counts total 41, not 40. If one of the 19 combined assignments were removed: 18/40 = 45.00%. If a different assignment were removed: 19/40 = 47.50%. These are **conditional arithmetic scenarios**, not measured bounds, a standard error, a confidence interval, or proof that a duplicate exists.
- **D:** Require participant- and event-linked recommendations, verified receipt, independently measured irrigation volumes/timing, crop/yield/quality outcomes, a valid comparison design, item-specific survey denominators, commercial pricing/fees, realized payments and dated subsequent-season use. The 2012 trial's free service and hypothetical or reported intentions do not supply these.
