# R-01 → B-01: Correcting “80% usefulness” from a participation abstract
**ID:** R01-B01-C96-UTILITY | **Date:** 2026-10-29 | **Integration owner:** B-01 | **Status:** READY_FOR_REVIEW; not integrated/published.

**Primary institutional source:** [University of New England original publication record for Car et al. (2012)](https://rune.une.edu.au/entities/publication/68f9d1ef-6e62-415a-b831-0beb96df9853), DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2012.03.003.

**Source-grounded quantities:** 72 irrigators; 45 reported through the season; 13 reported for half the season; 14 never reported. Six of the thirteen later said they wished they had continued. The institutional abstract then infers “overall 80%” useful. These are not a survey item-response ledger or observed water-saving outcomes.

## Original, ready-to-integrate teaching passage
**A denominator can change the scientific claim.** In a farm-advisory trial, a count of participants who transmitted information for some portion of a season is not the same thing as a count who rated the service useful. The trial record describes 45 all-season reporters, 13 half-season reporters and 14 never-reporters among 72 irrigators. Therefore the share who sent at least some data is 58/72, or 80.56%, whereas the share who sent data throughout is 45/72, or 62.50%. Six of the thirteen partial-season reporters retrospectively wished they had not stopped; adding those six to the all-season group gives 51/72, or 70.83%. None of these arithmetic operations reconstructs a questionnaire's unknown usefulness denominator. The original authors' 80% usefulness characterization must be presented as their inference, not independently measured satisfaction or compliance. An absent inbound text message does not demonstrate that the grower ignored a recommendation. The appropriate teaching lesson is to define the construct, observation unit, response frame and missingness before assigning an adoption percentage.

## Exercise and answer key (source values, not a new field study)
1. Calculate all-season reporting, any reporting and all-season-plus-regret as proportions of the 72 initial irrigators.
2. Can the author-reported 80% be proven to be a direct survey rating? **No**: the accessible institutional abstract supplies no item-specific response ledger.
3. Can the 14 nonreporters be labelled noncompliant? **No**: receipt, actionable advice and independently observed irrigation action are unknown.
4. Is a 58/72 participation percentage an irrigation water-saving estimate? **No**: no calibrated outcome/counterfactual is established.

**Answers:** 45/72=0.6250; (45+13)/72=0.80556; (45+6)/72=0.70833. This example is source-level arithmetic and interpretation, **not** a randomized trial, paid adoption model or validated effect. The author-uploaded Car Table 5/6 discrepancies remain open and should be noted in any advanced exercise.
