## Claim-level editorial decisions

| Draft claim / issue | Source adjudication | Required edit |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh mushrooms in sealed non-perforated packaging can pose botulism risk | Health Canada describes anaerobic conditions and *C. botulinum* risk in airtight packaging | Retain, with source and distinction between hermetic and perforated packages |
| Two 3.0-mm holes are universally sufficient for any mushroom package | 2013 guidance mentions two 3.0-mm holes over a tray **or equivalent air exchange** as advisory design, not a universal validation across films, load, temperature, dimensions or jurisdictions | Reject universal engineering guarantee; present as dated advisory example only |
| All perforated fresh mushroom trays are legally required to stay at 4°C or less under the specific hermetic-package rule | The cited 4°C rule in Health Canada's mushroom guidance explicitly addresses **hermetically sealed, non-commercially sterile** packages | Separate legal hermetic controls from ordinary prudent refrigeration advice for perforated products |
| Bilingual refrigeration phrase can be copied indiscriminately | Health Canada's mushroom-specific guidance uses “Keep Refrigerated” / “Garder au froid”; CFIA's general low-acid hermetic labeling explanation uses “Keep Refrigerated” / “Garder réfrigéré” in its cited legal exception | Do not present the two French strings as interchangeable verbatim legal quotations. State which authority, date, product and legal context each comes from; have Canadian regulatory reviewer resolve final label wording |
| Every prepackaged fresh mushroom must carry an ordinary 90-day best-before date | CFIA general date-marking page lists a **prepackaged fresh-fruit-and-vegetable exemption**, while special hermetic/mushroom guidance has specific labeling discussion | Do not universalize the ordinary 90-day rule. Explain special package conditions and obtain product/jurisdiction-specific compliance review |
| A best-before date proves microbiological safety | CFIA distinguishes freshness/quality date marking from food safety | Reject; require documented safety validation and cold-chain controls where applicable |

## Suggested textbook-ready correction (source-limited)

“Fresh mushrooms must not be assumed safe in oxygen-excluding packaging solely because they look unspoiled. Canadian mushroom-specific guidance distinguishes hermetically sealed containers from visibly perforated film and describes a historical perforation example of two 3.0-mm holes or equivalent air exchange; this is **not** a universal package-design certification. Canadian label and temperature obligations depend on the actual package type and applicable rules. Before commercializing any package, verify the current Canadian requirements, intended distribution temperatures, gas permeability, shelf-life evidence and final bilingual label with a qualified regulatory specialist.”
