# Building a 60-day simulated trading practice plan

> **Moj Prop educational resource** · Simulated trading examples only. This is not investment advice, a guarantee of profit, a challenge pass, or a reward.

Sixty days can provide a useful review window for repeated decisions, provided the participant tracks rules rather than forcing trades to hit a schedule. This guide is an educational practice plan, not an official pass criterion.

## Days 1–7: prepare the process
Record the virtual balance, instruments, session times, loss limits and assumptions. Define a trade thesis, invalidation point and planned quantity before every action. Write a rule for declining a trade when its risk cannot be measured.

## Days 8–30: measure decisions
Record each session, including days with no trades. Track planned and actual outcomes separately. At the end of each week summarize net simulated profit or loss, worst day, maximum open exposure, rule breaches and examples of good decisions. Do not increase position size to recover losses.

## Days 31–50: reduce repeated mistakes
Classify violations by cause: inaccurate sizing, missing invalidation, overlapping exposure, costs or recordkeeping. Select one correction at a time and compare like-for-like observations. Do not remove negative sessions to improve a reported ratio.

## Days 51–60: prepare an honest review
Reconcile the complete journal with the applicable rulebook. Distinguish arithmetic checks from subjective review. Keep supporting records and questions for the operator. Do not assume a positive balance or one satisfied rule makes you eligible for a reward.

A daily checklist can be short: reference balance; planned loss; aggregate exposure; reason for entering or not entering; realized outcome; rule compliance; one improvement.
