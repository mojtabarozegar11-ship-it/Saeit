# Simulated vs live trading: what a virtual balance actually means

> **Moj Prop educational resource** · Simulated trading examples only. This is not investment advice, a guarantee of profit, a challenge pass, or a reward.

A simulated trading challenge measures decisions under a defined model. It does not necessarily place orders at a broker or create ownership of the balance shown. A virtual $1,000 balance is not $1,000 of withdrawable cash, and paying for an evaluation is not the same as depositing investment capital.

## What a simulator can and cannot measure
A well-defined exercise can record entry and exit prices, position sizes, loss limits and the concentration of gains. It can help participants identify inconsistent exposure. Real execution introduces uncertainty: spreads, partial fills, trading fees, funding costs, latency and liquidity may differ from assumptions.

| Feature | Simulated exercise | Live trading |
|---|---|---|
| Displayed balance | Virtual accounting reference | Actual account equity subject to broker terms |
| Orders | Modelled execution | Market or broker execution |
| Costs | Model assumptions | Actual charges and execution costs |
| Outcomes | Evaluation evidence | Real gains and losses |

## Before paying for an evaluation
Read the current rulebook, service scope, eligibility, refund policy and any reward conditions. Distinguish a conditional performance reward from a guarantee of future returns. Confirm the operator and use only the current verified invoicing route. Never disclose wallet recovery words or trading passwords.

Ask what happens at a failed evaluation, how violations are calculated and what evidence is needed to resolve a dispute. Do not infer that a simulated pass creates a live broker account.
