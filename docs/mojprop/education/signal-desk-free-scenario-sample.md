### Position sizing including a cost reserve

Assume a **virtual $1,000 practice balance**, not client capital. Set an illustrative loss budget of **1% = $10**, which is more conservative than the public 3% per-trade ceiling described on the [MOJ PROP rules page](https://mojplaywin.com/moj-prop/rules/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=organic_referral&utm_campaign=signal_sample_20261122). Define the risk in *synthetic $-equivalent units only*; **this is not a broker lot-size specification**.

| Calculation | Exact value |
|---|---:|
| Invented entry reference | 100 |
| Invented invalidation level | 98 |
| Invented objective | 104 |
| Difference from entry to invalidation, per synthetic unit | 2 |
| Difference from entry to objective, per synthetic unit | 4 |
| Maximum teaching risk budget | $10 |
| Assumed combined fees/spread/friction reserve for the complete example | $2 |
| Max whole hypothetical units, `floor(($10 − $2) / $2)` | **4 units** |
| Planned downside, `4 × $2 + $2` | **$10** |
| Illustrative upside *before* costs, `4 × $4` | **$16** |
| Illustrative upside *after* the assumed $2 cost reserve | **$14** |
| Illustrative net reward / planned downside, `$14 / $10` | **1.4 : 1** |

The arithmetic is not a prediction. Real markets have different tick values, contract multipliers, spreads, swaps, slippage, partial fills and gap risk. A stop may execute beyond its level or not fill at all. The $2 friction number is an assumption for this **fabricated** example; it is not a verified broker quote. No real order size may be derived from this table without a venue-specific contract definition, current pricing and independent suitability review.

As a separate budget illustration, 4% of a *$1,000 reference amount* is $40 of aggregate planned open risk, and 6% is $60 of a hypothetical daily drawdown reference. These simple percentages do not reproduce all the evaluation engine's timing, equity/reference-balance or loss-definition rules. The applicable published terms govern. **Neither a hypothetical calculation nor a good risk ratio establishes eligibility, a challenge pass or a reward.**

## What a genuine researched brief must add before it is published

1. **Traceable data**: UTC publication timestamp, exact instrument/venue, data timestamp and independent source. Mark delayed or missing data; never substitute fictional quotes for market data.
2. **Falsifiable thesis**: explain the condition, alternative scenario, invalidation and a no-trade outcome without hindsight or a guaranteed direction.
3. **Realistic risk accounting**: contract multiplier, position value, estimated fees/spread, downside with slippage/stress, correlated exposure and open-loss budget; reject inputs that cannot be verified.
4. **Status and change control**: `draft`, `published`, `invalidated`, `expired`, `closed`, plus immutable revisions and explicit timestamps. Never backdate an observation or delete adverse outcomes.
5. **Customer transparency**: service coverage, delivery frequency, price, permitted jurisdictions, cancellation/support terms and whether anything is actually operational. Do not imply automatic delivery, profitability or regulatory authorisation without evidence.
