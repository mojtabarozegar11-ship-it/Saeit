## Executive observation

For gold, a single-factor “real yields up, gold down” framework is not sufficient in a market where official-sector purchases, exchange-traded investment flows, geopolitical risk and dollar liquidity may pull in different directions. A useful research service should make these competing channels explicit before reporting a directional hypothesis.

In its **3 November 2026** Q3 gold demand outlook, the World Gold Council described both headwinds from yields and the dollar and support from investment and official-sector demand. Its Q4 conclusions are expectations, not confirmed Q4 outcomes. The IMF's 2026 analysis of reserve gold similarly evaluates interactions among real-rate opportunity costs, safe-asset demand and currency effects. We do not reproduce their chart data or claim to own their research.

## Scenario matrix — conditions rather than forecasts

### A. Real-rate and dollar headwind scenario

**Conditional mechanism:** if *observed* longer-dated U.S. real yields rise persistently and dollar strength broadens, the comparative opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset rises. This may pressure gold investment demand, but the response need not be immediate or monotonic.

**Evidence required:** source-stamped Treasury/FRED yields; an appropriately licensed, dated broad-dollar measure; contemporaneous gold ETF flows where publication rights permit.

**What would weaken this scenario?** Continued investment inflows, stronger official-sector demand or a jump in safety demand despite rising yields. A single data point does not establish a persistent trend.

### B. Risk and reserve diversification support scenario

**Conditional mechanism:** market stress, reserve diversification motives or rising demand for liquid safety assets may offset the real-yield channel. The IMF and World Gold Council analyses provide economic context for why correlations can change across episodes.

**Evidence required:** a dated material risk event, published demand flows, cross-asset stress indicators and subsequent independent observations. Do not treat social-media claims as official central-bank purchases.

**What would weaken this scenario?** Broad outflows or a return to risk-taking without corroborating official-sector buying; the absence of timely verifiable data also invalidates a confident conclusion.

### C. Mixed evidence — no-direction conclusion

If real-yield, dollar, investment-flow and official-sector evidence conflict, or any essential dataset is stale, the appropriate published conclusion is **“no supported directional view.”** A research brief must be allowed to say no-trade/no-call. This is a quality control, not a trading outcome.
