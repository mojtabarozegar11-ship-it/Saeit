# What does a best-day consistency rule measure?

> **Moj Prop educational resource** · Simulated trading examples only. This is not investment advice, a guarantee of profit, a challenge pass, or a reward.

A consistency rule evaluates the concentration of a participant's gains across days. It is not an estimate of future returns. A participant can have positive net results and still have concentrated gains.

## A worked example
Consider five simulated daily results: +$30, +$20, -$10, +$15, +$5. Net profit is $60; the best day is $30. The best-day contribution is 30/60 = 50%. Under an illustrative rule limiting the best day to at most half of positive net profit, this example meets the ratio exactly. Other evaluation conditions may still apply.

Now change the -$10 day to -$20 while keeping the other outcomes. Net profit becomes $50; the best day remains $30. The ratio becomes 60%. The concentration rises even though the best day has not changed.

When cumulative profit is zero or negative, a positive-profit ratio is not a meaningful pass condition. The exact evaluation engine and published terms determine which sessions and profits count. Do not extrapolate these examples to a legal entitlement, payout or broker account.

## Practical use
Review the concentration measure as a diagnostic rather than a reason to place additional trades. Record every session, including losses and days without positions. Avoid increasing risk merely to change the denominator. Look for repeatable decisions, consistent risk limits and a clear stop process.

## Why simulation matters
A simulated balance is virtual. Transaction costs, market fills and available liquidity may differ from a real broker environment. Neither a positive journal nor compliance with one ratio guarantees a pass, a profit or a payment.
