# Buying a digital download with a TRX invoice: a safety and delivery checklist

A cryptocurrency invoice can be convenient, but blockchain transfers are usually irreversible. Before paying for a digital product, confirm that the seller, invoice, receiving network and delivery terms are clear. A product page being visible does not by itself prove that the full payment-to-download process has been independently tested.

This guide is general buyer education and does not constitute financial advice.

## 1. Start at the official product page

Open the seller's website directly or through a link you trust. Read the product title, edition, file format, license, current price and any device requirements. If a social post and the official page disagree, use the official page as the current reference and contact support before paying.

Avoid links sent by strangers claiming to provide a special payment address. A legitimate invoice should be tied to the specific order.

## 2. Understand what the invoice is asking you to pay

A USD-listed product may be converted into an exact TRX amount at checkout. Check the invoice's stated amount, network, receiving address, expiration conditions and any additional network fees. Do not assume that the USD price is itself the amount of TRX to send.

If the invoice has expired or changed, create or request a new one through the official site rather than guessing the amount.

## 3. Confirm the network and address

TRX is the native asset of the TRON network. Other assets or tokens with similar names are not interchangeable. Confirm that your wallet is sending the correct asset on the specified network. Copy the complete address from the active invoice and compare its beginning and ending characters after pasting.

Do not rely solely on a QR code or a previously used address. A receiving address can vary by order or merchant configuration.

## 4. Keep wallet credentials private

No seller needs your recovery phrase, seed words or private keys to receive a blockchain payment. Do not enter those credentials into a checkout form, email, direct message or support chat. Be cautious of requests to install remote-access software or to pay an unexpected “unlock” charge.

A normal payment from a self-custody wallet should be authorized in your own wallet interface.

## 5. Record the transaction evidence

After sending, retain the order identifier, invoice details, blockchain transaction hash and timestamp. Use a reputable TRON blockchain explorer to check network status, but remember that a confirmed blockchain transfer and a merchant-issued download entitlement are separate events.

If the site has not recognized the payment, contact the official support route with the order ID and public transaction hash. Never send your recovery phrase or unnecessary personal data.

## 6. Check delivery and file integrity

A reliable digital-product workflow should identify the purchased item, grant access only to the correct buyer and allow the customer to retrieve the correct file. After download, confirm that the file opens in a compatible application and matches the stated format. If a checksum is supplied, compare it with the downloaded file using a trusted local utility.

Keep a copy of the invoice and the seller's support instructions. Do not redistribute a licensed file unless its terms permit it.

## 8. Stop rather than guess

If an invoice does not show a complete address, the asset or network is ambiguous, or the file-delivery terms cannot be understood, do not send funds until the issue is resolved. A transparent checkout should make the next step understandable without requiring the customer to improvise.
