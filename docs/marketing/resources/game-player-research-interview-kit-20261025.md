# Browser-game player research: interview and observation kit
Free original research worksheet · MojPlayWin · 25 October 2026

**Purpose.** Identify actual player comprehension, frustration and replay motivations before making commercial claims. This is a field instrument, not evidence that any interviews have been conducted.

## Participant and consent
Recruit a diverse, voluntary sample of players matching the intended audience. Explain the activity, duration, recording policy and right to stop; obtain appropriate consent. Do not collect names, addresses, passwords, wallet details, health information or children's data. For minors, use appropriate guardian and legal safeguards. Assign random participant codes.

## Unmoderated task card
Record participant code, game/build identifier, device, OS, browser, input type, timestamp, screen resolution and network type. Give the following tasks without coaching:
1. Start a new session and explain the goal in your own words.
2. Complete the first challenge without help.
3. Locate pause, sound and accessibility controls.
4. Explain what happens after losing and whether progress persists.
5. Find a replay or retry path and decide whether you would play again.

For each task capture: completion (yes/no), elapsed seconds, number of failed attempts, help requested (yes/no), exact observed breakdown, and participant's own words. Separate observation from interpretation.

## Interview questions (neutral, not leading)
- What did you expect would happen when you first pressed Start?
- Which instruction was unclear, and at what moment?
- What part felt most satisfying? What made it satisfying?
- Was any interaction difficult on your device?
- When would you choose to stop playing?
- What would make you return voluntarily?
- Which feature, if any, would you share with someone else?
- Was any purchase or advertising message confusing?

## Evidence and analysis
For each issue, record severity 1–4 (cosmetic, friction, blocks optional task, blocks core play), reproduction steps, affected devices and supporting observation. Report **n/N** for task success; do not present a small convenience sample as population-representative. Median task time is more robust than a single best run. Count unique problems, not complaints.

## Release decision
Block release for repeatable crashes, inaccessible core controls, deceptive purchase prompts, lost purchases or personal-data leaks. Re-test fixes on actual target devices and keep the before/after evidence. No rating of 100/100 without defined external acceptance evidence.
