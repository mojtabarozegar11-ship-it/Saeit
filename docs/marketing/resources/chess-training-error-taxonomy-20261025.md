# Chess improvement: error taxonomy and weekly review
Free study resource · MojPlayWin · 25 October 2026

Use this taxonomy to turn game reviews into repeatable learning. No claim is made that it is extracted from, or included in, any commercial book.

## Eight mutually prioritized error categories
1. **Tactical oversight:** missed forcing move, hanging piece or tactical motif.
2. **Calculation failure:** candidate moves identified but lines not checked deeply enough.
3. **Threat blindness:** opponent's immediate plan or forcing reply overlooked.
4. **Opening understanding:** memorized moves used without knowing strategic goals.
5. **Positional evaluation:** incorrect assessment of pawn structure, king safety or piece activity.
6. **Endgame technique:** opposition, activity, conversion or drawing method misunderstood.
7. **Time management:** critical decision made with inappropriate time allocation.
8. **Decision discipline:** move played without a final blunder check or after emotional tilt.

Assign **one primary cause** and optional secondary tags to each critical error to avoid double counting.

## Review protocol
Within 24 hours, reconstruct the position *without an engine* and write three candidate moves, the expected opponent reply and the evaluation. Only then compare with a reputable analysis engine or stronger player's review. Record FEN or move number, clock time, actual move, better candidate, reason for difference and a concrete drill.

## Weekly metrics
- Critical errors per 100 moves = 100 × number of verified critical errors / total moves reviewed.
- Time-pressure errors = errors committed below a predeclared clock threshold / all errors.
- Training completion = completed focused sessions / scheduled focused sessions.
Report the sample size, time control and opponent strength distribution; do not compare blitz and classical games as if identical.

## Four-week correction loop
Week 1: establish baseline from 5–10 games. Week 2: isolate the two most frequent primary error categories. Week 3: solve positions with written candidate lines, then test transfer in real games. Week 4: re-measure with comparable games and retain failures for the next cycle.

**Progress criterion:** fewer verified recurring errors in a comparable sample, not merely a higher puzzle score.
