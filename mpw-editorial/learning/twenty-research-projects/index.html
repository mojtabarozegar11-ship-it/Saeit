Twenty Student Research Projects

A practical workbook: 20 distinct, safe and evidence-led mini-projects for school and university learners. Each activity has an original question, a concrete data plan, a defensible analysis and an ethical boundary.

Reader notice: Every case is a teaching design, not a report of completed field research. No measurements, survey responses or business results have been fabricated. Obtain applicable school, institutional and participant permissions before collecting real data. No payment or live commerce operation is part of this workbook.

PROJECT 01

School garden water-use log

Middle school / environmental science

Research question

How does recorded weekly garden water use vary before and after a scheduling change?

Design

Eight-week before/after observation; compare four weeks per phase without claiming causality.

Evidence and units

Record liters from the same calibrated container or meter, watering date, rainfall, garden area and major plant changes. Use the same unit throughout.

Action sequence

Write the meter-reading rule; collect the initial four weekly totals; make a time graph; document the scheduling change; collect the next four; annotate weather differences.

Analysis and limits

Compare mean liters/week and absolute difference. Compute percentage change only when the baseline mean is positive. Separate observed reduction from an explanation of its cause.

Ethics and safety

Use no student identities, photographs or unauthorized access to water controls; ask the facilities manager before measuring shared utilities.

Deliverable

An eight-row dataset, one line chart and a claim-limitation paragraph.

Quality question: Are units, weather and the baseline denominator visible?

PROJECT 02

Library waiting-time sampling

Secondary school / operations

Research question

At which observed time blocks is the library checkout queue longest?

Design

Repeated 10-minute time samples across two weekdays and two time blocks each day.

Evidence and units

Count people waiting at fixed timestamps, record the number of open service desks and note school events; do not record faces or names.

Action sequence

Create a four-block timetable, record five queue counts in each block, and mark any staff or timetable changes before summarizing.

Analysis and limits

Report each block's median count and range; a longer observed queue does not itself establish poor staff performance.

Ethics and safety

Obtain site permission; never obstruct access or track an individual's movements.

Deliverable

Twenty anonymized observations, a block comparison and a staffing question for discussion.

Quality question: Were every sampling interval and each open service desk documented?

PROJECT 03

Reading routine and recall

High school / learning science

Research question

How does one volunteer's immediate recall differ across two reading routines?

Design

An opt-in, within-person exploratory activity with comparable passages and randomized order.

Evidence and units

Record passage word counts, reading duration, question difficulty and a rubric-based recall score, without storing the participant's identity.

Action sequence

Prepare two independently written passages; set five equally weighted questions each; alternate routine order; record the score and participant reflections.

Analysis and limits

Compare score out of five and reading time; discuss order, familiarity and passage difficulty as confounders. Do not generalize from one person.

Ethics and safety

Voluntary participation and school rules apply; never grade or profile classmates without permission.

Deliverable

Two original passages, ten scored questions, one small comparison and a limitations note.

Quality question: Are the passages comparable and the voluntary participation clearly recorded?

PROJECT 04

Natural light in study spaces

High school / design

Research question

How does measured desk illumination change across three locations during a school day?

Design

Repeated, non-interventional lux readings at fixed desks and times.

Evidence and units

Use one illuminance meter, fixed measurement height, timestamp, weather category and any electric lights switched on.

Action sequence

Mark three permitted desks; take readings at opening, midday and late afternoon over two days; record shadows and lamp state.

Analysis and limits

Plot lux by time and desk; interpret variation without claiming medical or ergonomic compliance from a class project.

Ethics and safety

Do not collect personal comfort or medical information without consent; avoid looking into or modifying electrical fixtures.

Deliverable

An 18-cell observation matrix, labeled plot and practical follow-up measurement plan.

Quality question: Was sensor height kept constant and electric lighting annotated?

PROJECT 05

Compost mass-change observation

Middle / high school / biology

Research question

How does the mass of a sealed school-approved compost sample change over four weeks?

Design

Observational sample weighing; no handling of hazardous, unknown or clinical waste.

Evidence and units

Record container tare, sample mass, dates, storage conditions and whether material was added or removed.

Action sequence

Use a teacher-approved benign plant-material sample, define container and scale procedure, and log weekly readings without opening unsafe material.

Analysis and limits

Separate mass change from assumptions about decomposition; water evaporation and scale error may dominate.

Ethics and safety

Wear school-required protection; do not culture microbes, collect biological hazards or conduct unsupervised waste handling.

Deliverable

A four-date mass log and a short measurement-error analysis.

Quality question: Are container mass, added material and moisture loss kept distinct?

PROJECT 06

Packaging waste count

Middle school / sustainability

Research question

Which packaging categories are most common in a designated school collection period?

Design

Non-personal item count from an approved, safe observation point.

Evidence and units

Define paper, rigid plastic, flexible plastic, metal and other categories; count each item once with a reproducible rule.

Action sequence

Create a category sheet, agree on examples, observe the approved collection area on three days and reconcile ambiguous items as 'other'.

Analysis and limits

Calculate each category's share of the observed total and show absolute counts beside percentages.

Ethics and safety

Do not inspect private bags or handle dangerous or contaminated refuse; obtain site approval.

Deliverable

A coded tally sheet, frequency table and one feasible waste-prevention suggestion.

Quality question: Can a second student classify the same item using the stated definitions?

PROJECT 07

Cafeteria food-waste estimate

High school / resource management

Research question

What share of a permitted sample of prepared food was not served or consumed?

Design

Aggregate, staff-assisted weighing for two menu days; no individual plate tracking.

Evidence and units

Obtain staff-recorded prepared and residual mass with container tare, menu, date and serving count.

Action sequence

Agree on a hygienic collection boundary with staff, record two mass balances, and note whether leftovers were separately donated or retained.

Analysis and limits

Calculate residual/handled mass with a nonzero denominator; disclose that plate waste, kitchen trim and unsold portions are different categories.

Ethics and safety

No food sampling, direct handling or photos of pupils; follow kitchen safety policy.

Deliverable

Two aggregate mass-balance sheets, a category definition and one reduction hypothesis.

Quality question: Is every food-flow category distinguished before calculating a rate?

PROJECT 08

Arrival mode count

High school / geography

Research question

What transport modes are observed among arrivals at one school entrance during two short windows?

Design

Coarse anonymous counts from an authorized safe location, not an individual travel survey.

Evidence and units

Tally walking, cycling, bus, car and unclassified arrivals using an agreed counting rule.

Action sequence

Choose a 15-minute window on two days; mark overlapping arrival groups; record weather and any entrance closure.

Analysis and limits

Compare mode shares and absolute totals; account for multiple entrances and absences in limitations.

Ethics and safety

Never record plate numbers, precise routes, photos or identities; avoid road hazards.

Deliverable

Two anonymous tallies and a diagram of the sampling frame.

Quality question: Does the study avoid suggesting that observed mode share represents all pupils?

PROJECT 09

Household appliance energy model

High school / applied mathematics

Research question

Which listed appliance contributes most to modeled weekly electricity use under stated assumptions?

Design

Desk-based model using published nameplate wattage and illustrative usage hours.

Evidence and units

Make a four-appliance table with watts, assumed hours/day and days/week; label every figure as an assumption or verified nameplate reading.

Action sequence

Use ordinary unpowered manufacturer information, calculate watt-hours and kilowatt-hours, and vary usage time by plus or minus 20%.

Analysis and limits

Energy = watts x hours / 1000; compare modeled contributions and sensitivity. This is not a measured bill or safety audit.

Ethics and safety

Do not open sockets, measure live wiring or reveal a household's personal utility records.

Deliverable

Four-row model, sensitivity table and uncertainty statement.

Quality question: Are watts, hours and kWh correctly distinguished?

PROJECT 10

Room air-quality measurement design

University foundation / environmental measurement

Research question

How repeatable are low-cost CO2 sensor readings in two ordinary ventilated rooms?

Design

Observational instrument comparison at the same locations and times, not a health intervention.

Evidence and units

Record device model, warm-up, readings, room occupancy category, ventilation state and time.

Action sequence

Document calibration instructions, collect three repeated readings per room at two sessions, then compute within-session spread.

Analysis and limits

Compare repeatability and sensor drift; explain why CO2 alone cannot diagnose overall air quality or clinical risk.

Ethics and safety

No names, medical claims or modification of building systems; request room access and comply with ventilation policy.

Deliverable

Twelve readings, range/median comparison and a measurement-quality memo.

Quality question: Is instrument uncertainty separated from real room variation?

PROJECT 11

Microbusiness break-even worksheet

High school / entrepreneurship

Research question

How many units must a hypothetical handmade notebook venture sell to cover specified costs?

Design

Transparent illustrative cost model, not a claim about actual prices or expected profit.

Evidence and units

List sample fixed costs, price/unit, variable cost/unit, spoilage and selling fees in a defined currency.

Action sequence

Choose two hypothetical prices, calculate contribution margin and break-even units, and test one higher materials-cost scenario.

Analysis and limits

Break-even units = fixed cost / positive unit contribution, rounded up; explain when contribution is zero or negative.

Ethics and safety

Do not solicit money, create listings or promise revenue; identify costs intentionally omitted from the model.

Deliverable

A reproducible calculation sheet and a three-scenario decision note.

Quality question: Were labor, fees and returns considered as potential omissions?

PROJECT 12

Craft-product price perception

University foundation / marketing research

Research question

How do hypothetical price scenarios change stated purchase interest for an unbranded craft item?

Design

Consent-based vignette survey with random order and no actual purchases.

Evidence and units

Record anonymous interest scores for three clearly labeled hypothetical prices, plus version and question order.

Action sequence

Write neutral descriptions, get institutional permission if recruiting, pilot comprehension with volunteers and summarize scores without identification.

Analysis and limits

Describe stated preference by scenario; do not infer actual price elasticity or market demand from intentions alone.

Ethics and safety

No minors' responses without applicable approval; no pressure, payments or collected contact information.

Deliverable

A three-price survey instrument, anonymous summary and validity caveat.

Quality question: Are price order effects and hypothetical-bias limitations disclosed?

PROJECT 13

Seed-germination comparison

Middle / high school / plant biology

Research question

What difference in germination counts appears across two safe light conditions for the same seed lot?

Design

Teacher-approved randomized comparison using non-invasive plant materials.

Evidence and units

Track seed lot, initial count per group, water schedule, temperature band, visible germination day and excluded seeds.

Action sequence

Allocate equal counts randomly, keep containers equivalent, record daily outcomes with a fixed germination definition, then stop at the agreed day.

Analysis and limits

Compare germinated/initial count with denominators; discuss small sample, seed viability and moisture as competing explanations.

Ethics and safety

Use locally permitted, non-invasive plants; avoid treated seeds, hazardous chemicals and release into the environment.

Deliverable

Allocation record, daily count table and cautious comparison graph.

Quality question: Does the analysis preserve ungerminated seeds in the denominator?

PROJECT 14

Soil-moisture reading agreement

High school / agriculture

Research question

How consistent are duplicate moisture-probe readings within three permitted garden zones?

Design

Repeated measurement agreement study, not a prescription for irrigation.

Evidence and units

Record sensor ID, approximate zone, depth, two readings, time since watering and weather notes.

Action sequence

Choose three safe zones, collect duplicate readings without changing irrigation, and repeat after one ordinary watering event.

Analysis and limits

Compute paired differences and their spread; distinguish device repeatability from site heterogeneity.

Ethics and safety

Obtain gardening permission, avoid buried cables and do not alter watering for the sake of this class exercise.

Deliverable

Six paired observations, agreement plot and a sensor-placement checklist.

Quality question: Were depth, probe ID and time since watering held visible?

PROJECT 15

Rainfall collection potential

High school / civil-environmental modeling

Research question

What annual collection volume might a hypothetical roof provide under three rainfall scenarios?

Design

Scenario calculation from roof area, credible regional precipitation sources and an assumed collection coefficient.

Evidence and units

Record area in square meters, rainfall in millimeters, model coefficient and exact source/date for each scenario.

Action sequence

Define low/central/high inputs, compute volume in liters, and run a separate roof-area sensitivity calculation.

Analysis and limits

Liters = area m2 x rainfall mm x coefficient; highlight that useful supply also depends on storage, losses, water quality and legality.

Ethics and safety

Do not climb roofs, connect plumbing or claim potability or a permitted installation.

Deliverable

Three-scenario workbook and a limitations diagram.

Quality question: Are roof area and precipitation units traceable to their sources?

PROJECT 16

Educational product-page usability

University / interaction design

Research question

Can consenting adult testers find the format, price and refund terms on a mock product page?

Design

Task-based evaluation on an offline mockup without live payment or personal accounts.

Evidence and units

For each task, record success/failure, time-to-find and a categorized obstacle without identities or recordings by default.

Action sequence

Create three neutral findability tasks, obtain consent, allow withdrawal, run at least one pilot and revise ambiguous instructions before assessment.

Analysis and limits

Summarize per-task completion as count/total and list blockers ranked by frequency, avoiding claims about all customers.

Ethics and safety

Do not collect payment details, require a wallet connection or expose real customer orders.

Deliverable

Three tasks, anonymous usability sheet and a prioritized page-improvement proposal.

Quality question: Are the mockup and live commerce paths explicitly distinguished?

PROJECT 17

Game onboarding comprehension

High school / game design

Research question

Which instruction in a paper prototype is most often misunderstood by opt-in adult peers?

Design

Offline paper-prototype walkthrough, not live behavioral tracking.

Evidence and units

Count comprehension of three unique instructions, note a neutral observer's rubric and a brief anonymous quote only with permission.

Action sequence

Prepare an original mini-game with three actions, give each tester the same brief, ask them to explain each action, then revise one ambiguous instruction.

Analysis and limits

Compare before/after comprehension counts only if the same rubric is preserved; avoid overclaiming with a small convenience sample.

Ethics and safety

No minors' profiling, financial rewards, coercion, microphone recording or retention of personal profiles.

Deliverable

Paper cards, comprehension rubric and a versioned instruction revision.

Quality question: Does the revision change only the instruction being evaluated?

PROJECT 18

Synthetic ranking-fairness audit

University / data ethics

Research question

Does a rule-based fictional scholarship ranking behave differently for two synthetic applicant groups?

Design

Fully synthetic dataset; transparent scoring rule with counterfactual pairs.

Evidence and units

Generate 12 fabricated records with study score, fictional group label, selection threshold and documented rule; mark every record synthetic.

Action sequence

Calculate selected counts for each group, form pairs differing only in a group marker, and compare outputs under the original and revised rule.

Analysis and limits

Report selection rates with denominators, note instability from small synthetic samples and avoid declaring a real system discriminatory.

Ethics and safety

Use no real applicant or protected personal data; fairness requires contextual evaluation beyond this demonstration.

Deliverable

Synthetic table, disclosed scoring formula and a counterfactual comparison.

Quality question: Can another reader reproduce every ranking from the stated rule?

PROJECT 19

Source-tracing a public claim

High school / media literacy

Research question

How much of one non-sensitive public claim is supported by the earliest accessible primary source?

Design

Document-trail analysis with a fixed claim, publication dates and source hierarchy.

Evidence and units

Capture the exact short claim, publisher, date, cited intermediary and original document URL with an access date.

Action sequence

Trace at most three citation hops, compare headline wording with source scope, and label any unsupported expansion or missing original.

Analysis and limits

Classify the specific wording as supported, partly supported or unverified; avoid a sweeping judgment about the whole publisher.

Ethics and safety

Avoid private rumors, personal accusations and sharing paywalled or copyrighted full texts.

Deliverable

A provenance diagram, two short compliant excerpts and a claim-evidence table.

Quality question: Does the conclusion match the original source rather than the intermediary headline?

PROJECT 20

Oral-history consent and context

University / humanities

Research question

What can one explicitly consented personal recollection reveal, and what remains unverified?

Design

Interview-method planning, with the default deliverable being an unfilled consent protocol rather than a live interview.

Evidence and units

Prepare five open-ended questions, recording choices, retention window, withdrawal process and a separate public-quotation permission.

Action sequence

Draft a consent form and test whether a hypothetical answer can be labeled recollection, documentary fact or interpretation; do not solicit real testimony without approval.

Analysis and limits

Show how a recollection could be contextualized with independent historical records; never assign representativeness from one account.

Ethics and safety

No recording or publishing identifiable testimony without informed consent and institutional safeguards when applicable.

Deliverable

A blank consent form, interview guide, provenance matrix and ethics decision log.

Quality question: Are interview permission and publication/translation/commercial permission separate?

Editorial and learner checklist

  1. Define the target population and sampling frame before collecting values.
  2. Preserve units, instrument settings, dates, missing observations and source links.
  3. Separate observations, calculations, explanations and recommendations.
  4. Never treat hypothetical calculations as real measurements or guaranteed outcomes.
  5. Obtain necessary permission, minimize personal information and follow applicable safety rules.
  6. Show denominators and uncertainty; identify what the study cannot establish.
