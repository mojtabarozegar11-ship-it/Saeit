A risk-first prop-program blueprint

An illustrative decision and controls document that shows the structure of a paid, client-specific Rulebook engagement. It is not an offer of a trading challenge, funded account or investment contract.

Sample only. No live trader onboarding, brokerage execution, deposits, payouts, or capital allocations are authorized. Example thresholds are placeholders requiring legal, broker, treasury and risk approval.

1. Program objective and scope

The intended service is to design the governance artifacts for a simulated trading evaluation. The deliverable includes written eligibility rules, execution-data schema, monitoring responsibilities, dispute handling, and a decision log. Evaluation contracts or real funding must not start without documented entity, jurisdiction, identity, consumer-protection and treasury checks.

These figures are examples for documentation and arithmetic review, not production account terms. The definition of loss must explicitly account for commissions, swaps, slippage, currency conversion, positions across day boundaries and floating P/L. Otherwise the same account can be marked pass/fail inconsistently.

3. Decision-state machine

Draft → Identity review → Simulated evaluation → Pending independent audit → Pass / Fail / Dispute → Closed.

Every transition requires an actor, timestamp, rule version, immutable input digest and reason code. Real money and payout actions are outside this sample engine.

4. Separation of duties

Operations publishes versioned written terms. Risk owns loss definitions and independent recalculation. Finance validates available funds before any proposed payout. Compliance resolves jurisdiction and consumer restrictions. Support can open an appeal but cannot override trade history or approve itself. A single AI agent may prepare evidence, never self-approve an exception that changes client money or contractual rights.

5. Deliverable acceptance criteria

  • Every field in the rulebook maps to a deterministic machine-readable calculation and a plain-language explanation.
  • Boundary cases are documented: exactly-at-limit, late-price events, partial fills, missing external broker evidence, and clock rollover.
  • Redacted replay fixtures produce identical outcomes from the same historical inputs.
  • No production claim is made until signed contracts, independent acceptance tests and secure runtime evidence exist.
  • Any proposed offer includes scope, price, delivery date, revision policy and a lawful invoice route before customer payment.

6. Buyer handoff

A client-specific paid engagement would provide: editable rulebook; mathematical specification and formula table; simulated event replay; implementation backlog; risk register; compliance checklist; and a dated change log. This public sample contains none of the customer's sensitive data.
