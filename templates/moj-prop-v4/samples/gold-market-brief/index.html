1. Market structure and evidence baseline

Gold provides no cash coupon, so changes in expected real yields can alter its opportunity cost; U.S.-dollar movements affect the quoted dollar price, while physical and investment demand create additional forces. The IMF's 2026 analysis of gold in central-bank reserves distinguishes real-rate, convenience-yield and U.S.-dollar channels. The World Gold Council's mid-2026 outlook also treats interest-rate expectations and the dollar as important but insufficient standalone predictors. These are driver hypotheses, not mechanically guaranteed correlations.

The COMEX GC futures contract is 100 troy ounces; the MGC micro contract is 10 troy ounces. Different contract size, margin, expiry, liquidity and delivery rules mean a price move cannot be translated into a customer risk amount without first specifying the instrument and broker conditions. CME is the source of the contract specification; the numbers here are structural facts, not 2026-11-15 quotations.

2. Three falsifiable research scenarios

Scenario A — Lower real yields

Hypothesis: A sustained decline in inflation-adjusted yields reduces the holding-cost disadvantage of non-yielding gold.

Evidence required: Dated U.S. real-yield series, dollar index, validated gold spot/futures prices and comparable market sessions.

Falsification: Real yields fall but gold fails to confirm across matched closes, or liquidity/spread distortions dominate.

Scenario B — Higher real yields

Hypothesis: A persistent rise in real yields and broad dollar strength may pressure gold, all else equal.

Evidence required: Surprise vs consensus in U.S. data, real yields, dollar moves and gold's response after the same event timestamps.

Falsification: Strong safe-asset or physical demand offsets the expected response, or real yields reverse.

Scenario C — Conflicting signals

Hypothesis: Macro drivers diverge or market depth deteriorates; no directional conclusion is justified.

Evidence required: Volatility, bid/ask spread, venue hours, open interest, trade volumes and an explicit data-quality gate.

Falsification: Later independent snapshots resolve the divergence and satisfy predeclared verification rules.
