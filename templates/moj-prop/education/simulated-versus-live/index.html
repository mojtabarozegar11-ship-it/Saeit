Simulated trading is not live broker execution

Understand the important distinction between a practice evaluation, an operator-funded performance reward and ownership of actual brokerage capital.

Simulation and reward disclosure: This library provides education, not investment advice or a guarantee of earnings. The advertised $1,000 balance is virtual; no customer trading capital is accepted. Any real performance reward is conditional on verified qualifying profit after a successful evaluation and complete accepted terms. Payment and payout functionality must be verified independently before sending funds.

What a virtual account means

A simulated $1,000 balance is an accounting reference for an evaluation. It is not a customer's deposit, an investable cash balance or an amount available to withdraw. MOJ PROP describes both its evaluation and post-pass account activity as simulated; purchasing entry does not establish a personal funded brokerage account.

Why hypothetical performance has limitations

Simulated performance can differ substantially from live results because it may not fully capture latency, market impact, unavailable liquidity, slippage or how real financial exposure changes decisions. The U.S. CFTC's regulatory discussion of hypothetical performance illustrates why claims must identify these limitations. That reference is educational context, not a claim that a particular jurisdiction or regulation governs this service.

What a conditional reward is

The currently advertised MOJ PROP policy describes a real operator-funded performance reward at a 100% rate of verified qualifying trader-earned profit after a successful evaluation. This does not mean the virtual principal is withdrawn or that a pass, profit amount, payout date or live treasury settlement has already been proved. Before any purchase, independently verify the published eligibility, accepted payout terms, settlement method and available evidence.
