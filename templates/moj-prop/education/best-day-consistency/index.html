The best-day consistency ratio explained

A concentration rule asks whether a single simulated trading day accounts for too much of the net gain. It is not a forecast of profitability.

How the 50% rule is described

Under the currently advertised MOJ PROP Starter 30 summary, the best trading day must contribute no more than 50% of counted net simulated profit at evaluation. All other assessment terms continue to apply. The final accepted rulebook must specify exactly which days, fees and outcomes are counted.

Illustrative best-day share = largest counted profitable day ÷ positive counted net simulated profit × 100

Example A: exactly at the 50% threshold

Consider five simulated daily outcomes of +$30, +$20, −$10, +$15 and +$5. Net profit is $60. The best day is $30. The ratio is $30 ÷ $60 = 50%. In this numerical illustration the threshold is met exactly, but no other program criterion has been assessed.

Example B: an additional loss changes the ratio

Keep the best day at +$30, but replace the −$10 session with −$20. Net profit falls to $50 and the ratio rises to 60%. Even without a new winning session, concentration can worsen when cumulative net profit shrinks.

Example C: zero or negative net profit

When the countable net result is zero or negative, division cannot serve as a positive-profit consistency pass. The final evaluation system must specify how that situation is classified. Do not manufacture extra trades to manipulate the denominator.

What the rule does not prove

A ratio at or below 50% alone does not mean a challenge is passed, a participant has earned a payment, or live-market results will match the simulation. Loss limits, minimum activity, permitted strategies and other published criteria are independent checks. Any performance reward applies only to independently verified eligible profit after successful assessment, under complete published terms.

A better practice

Use a daily journal with realized and unrealized simulation results clearly separated. Review cumulative net profit and the largest counted day at the same checkpoint. Prefer reproducible entries with measured downside over rushed trades designed to change the score.
