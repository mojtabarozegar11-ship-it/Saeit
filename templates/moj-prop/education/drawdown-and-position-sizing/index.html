Drawdown and position sizing, step by step

Turn percentages into planned simulated loss amounts and understand why separate risk limits must never be combined into a single allowance.

1. The position-size calculation

For a simple instrument whose value changes by $1 per unit for each $1 price move, an illustrative sizing rule is:

Units = (maximum planned loss − estimated costs) ÷ (absolute entry-to-stop distance × dollar value per point per unit)

If a $1,000 virtual reference account has a 3% trade ceiling, the maximum indicated risk is $30. For a $2 stop distance and a $1-per-point unit value, the cost-free calculation produces 15 units. With $2 of estimated execution costs, the budget falls to $28 and the position becomes 14 units. Contract sizes, tick values and fees can change this calculation considerably. Never treat it as an order recommendation.

3. Daily drawdown is not a trade-size permission

Six percent of $1,000 is $60, but that number does not grant permission for a single $60 risk. The independently applicable 3% per-trade and 4% aggregate ceilings remain. A rolling or daily loss calculation may include both closed losses and open losses, depending on the final terms.

4. What about total drawdown?

The public Starter 30 risk summary does not fully settle all overall-drawdown reference definitions and daily reset details. A participant should require the complete accepted terms before payment. Do not infer that a past draft or a generic prop-firm convention is the current enforceable rule.

5. A conservative decision sequence

  1. Write down the correct reference balance and current simulated equity.
  2. Read the remaining daily capacity under the official calculation.
  3. Calculate planned loss at the invalidation price, including costs.
  4. Sum all open-position planned losses and compare with the aggregate ceiling.
  5. Decline orders if any independent limit is exceeded.
  6. Log the outcome and assumptions for independent review.

These examples teach arithmetic and decision discipline; they do not establish a pass, eligibility for a payment, or expected real trading returns.
